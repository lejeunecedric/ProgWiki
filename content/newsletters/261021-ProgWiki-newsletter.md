## New releases this week

Fresh finds in this week's music papers — all spotted in the Dutch Progressive
Rock Page's latest review issues:

**Lonely Robot — An Ending** (2026, UK) — John Mitchell closes the six-album
astronaut arc of his solo project with what DPRP's round-table calls best
album of 2026 territory: soaring Gilmour-style guitar, mellotron shading in
memory of Tony Banks, and a goodbye sung in earnest. Reviewed this week,
with an interview of the man himself.
([DPRP 2026-048](https://www.dprp.net/reviews/2026/048))

**Haken — In A Fever Dream** (2026, UK) — five tracks of heavy prog and
prog-metal, the band's first new music since guitarist Charlie Griffiths and
bassist Conner Green left in January. Reviewed this week by DPRP.
([DPRP 2026-047](https://www.dprp.net/reviews/2026/047))

**Charles Brown — Wind Of The Northern Light** (2026, USA) — an instrumental
guitar album with a prog heart: Roland GR-30 synth textures, Hackett-ish
atmosphere, and a ten-minute centrepiece. Reviewed this week by DPRP.
([DPRP 2026-047](https://www.dprp.net/reviews/2026/047))

**Forgotten Roads — Scenes From A Revolution (Original Soundtrack)** (2026,
USA) — 68 minutes telling the Russian Revolution through one family's flight
west, sung like a radio play, with a museum of a website giving an essay to
every track. Reviewed this week by DPRP.
([DPRP 2026-047](https://www.dprp.net/reviews/2026/047))

**Fiesta Alba — Drops Of Sunshine In The City Of Spectres** (2026, Italy) —
a 23-minute EP of math-rock, Afrobeat minimalism and experimental psychedelia
that ends in King Crimson-like dissonance. Reviewed this week by DPRP.
([DPRP 2026-047](https://www.dprp.net/reviews/2026/047))

## Special

**This issue's special: Why is King Crimson the yardstick of serious prog?** (query)

**Question:** Why is King Crimson so often treated as the yardstick of "serious" progressive rock? **1. They defined the genre's founding template.** King Crimson's 1969 debut *In the Court of the Crimson King* is widely cited by music historians as a definitive cornerstone of progressive rock, and the band are regarded as a foundational catalyst of the movement. Pete Townshend called the album "an uncanny masterpiece". **2. They combined rock power with classical and jazz precision.** "21st Century Schizoid Man" was described by *Rolling Stone* as "a seven-and-a-half-minute statement of purpose: rock power, jazz spontaneity, and classical precision harnessed in the service of a common aim".

## Anniversaries

### Many happy returns, Jon Anderson

Born 1944 — turning **82** this week. Jon Anderson (born John Roy Anderson, 25 October 1944) is an English singer, songwriter and musician who co-founded the progressive rock band Yes with bassist Chris Squire in 1968 and rose to prominence as their lead vocalist. Known for his high tenor vocal style, Anderson was a member of Yes across three tenures: 1968 to 1980, 1983 to 1988, and 1990 to 2008.
