# Analogue Synthesizer Texture

**Analogue synthesizer texture** is the use of the synthesizer not as a lead instrument but as **atmosphere, pad and vibrato** — sustained, shimmering, wash-like layers of sound that colour a piece rather than carry its melody. It is one of the defining sonic signatures of progressive rock, and it is what separates the synth's *textural* role from its *solo* role.

## How it manifests in prog
Instead of a single melodic line, the analogue synth is used to build **dense, evolving beds of sound**: slow filter sweeps, detuned oscillators, vibrato and portamento glides, and long sustained chords that swell and recede. The [[moog-synthesizer]]'s voltage-controlled oscillators and filters, and the Minimoog's warm, rich, never-quite-synchronised oscillators, are ideal for this — producing the "shimmer" and "wash" that became prog's atmospheric trademark. ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-moog-synthesizer.md] ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-minimoog.md]

## Canonical examples
- **The Dark Side of the Moon** (Pink Floyd, 1973) — its shimmering, atmospheric synth textures (alongside tape loops and the EMS VCS 3 / Synthi A) are the archetype of the sound. ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-dark-side-of-the-moon.md]
- **Rick Wakeman** (Yes) — choir-like pads and sweeping textures layered over the band's arrangements.
- **Keith Emerson** (ELP) — dramatic, expressive synth leads and textures.
- **Cousins:** Jean-Michel Jarre and Tangerine Dream pushed the same textural approach into pure electronic/ambient music — Tangerine Dream's sequencer-and-pad "cosmic music" is a direct descendant of the analogue-texture aesthetic. ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-tangerine-dream.md]

## Relationship to related concepts
Analogue synthesizer texture is the *sound* that the [[moog-synthesizer]] makes possible, and it is the natural companion of [[extended-composition]] — sustained pads need long-form space to develop. It also sits alongside the [[mellotron]]'s choral/string washes as the two great "atmosphere" keyboards of classic prog, and connects prog to the wider [[space-rock]] and [[krautrock]] scenes.
