# Virtuosity

**Virtuosity** is instrumental mastery — the display of technical ability and skill well above the average performer. A virtuoso is an individual who possesses outstanding talent and technical ability in a particular art or field.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]

## Definition
- The defining element of virtuosity is the performance ability of the musician, who is capable of displaying feats of skill well above the average performer.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]
- In music, both critics and musicians have mixed opinions on virtuosity: while the skill implied is clearly positive, musicians focused on virtuosity have been criticized for overlooking substance and emotion in favour of raw technical prowess.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]

## How it manifests in prog
- Prog made instrumental mastery a trademark: the genre's signature sound depends on players who can navigate abrupt changes of metre, key and texture within a single extended piece.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]
- The "show-off vs music-first" tension is central to prog's reception — the same technical display that defines the genre is also what critics attack as pretension or excess.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]

## Canonical examples
- **[[chris-squire|Chris Squire]]** ([[yes|Yes]]) — played his bass often in the higher register and developed a style involving "unusual beat placement" and time signatures.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]
- **[[steve-howe|Steve Howe]]** ([[yes|Yes]]) — established himself as an integral part of the group's sound with his Gibson ES-175 and a variety of acoustic guitars.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]
- **[[rick-wakeman|Rick Wakeman]]** ([[yes|Yes]]) — a classically trained player and noted studio musician; Squire said he could play "a grand piano for three bars, a Mellotron for two bars and a Moog for the next one absolutely spot on".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]
- **[[keith-emerson|Keith Emerson]]** ([[emerson-lake-palmer|ELP]]) — flamboyant use of the Hammond organ, Moog synthesizer and piano dominated ELP's sound.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]
- **[[carl-palmer|Carl Palmer]]** ([[emerson-lake-palmer|ELP]]) — the driving rock drummer of the ELP power trio.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]
- **[[bill-bruford|Bill Bruford]]** ([[yes|Yes]], [[king-crimson|King Crimson]]) — developed a style involving "unusual beat placement" and time signatures, allowing the drums to "be heard"; ranked No. 16 on *Rolling Stone*'s "100 Greatest Drummers of All Time".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]

## The tension: show-off vs music-first
Virtuosity in prog is double-edged. The same technical display that defines the genre is attacked as pretension — ELP, for instance, drew criticism for excess, with a popular 1970s joke being "How do you spell pretentious? E-L-P."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md] The debate echoes the wider historical one over virtuosity, in which Franz Liszt declared it "an indispensable element of music" while Richard Wagner opposed the "triviality and exhibitionist talents of the performer".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]

## Relationship to related concepts
Virtuosity is the performance engine of prog's [[extended-composition|extended compositions]] and [[suite|suites]], and is closely tied to the [[classical-and-jazz-crossover|classical and jazz crossover]], since prog's technical display often imports classical and jazz technique. It is also a defining trait of [[progressive-metal|progressive metal]], which showcases the extreme technical proficiency of its performers.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-virtuoso.md]
