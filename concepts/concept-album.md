# Concept Album

## Definition

A concept album is a musical album whose tracks hold a larger purpose or meaning collectively than they do individually, typically achieved through a single central narrative or theme, which can be instrumental, compositional, or lyrical. Roy Shuker defines concept albums and rock operas as albums that are "unified by a theme, which can be instrumental, compositional, narrative, or lyrical... In this form, the album changed from a collection of heterogeneous songs into a narrative work with a single theme, in which individual songs segue into one another."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-concept-album.md]

## How it manifests in prog

According to author Edward Macan, concept albums as a recurrent theme in progressive rock were directly inspired by the counterculture: "the consistent use of lengthy forms such as the programmatic song cycle of the concept album and the multimovement suite underscores the hippies' new, drug-induced conception of time."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-concept-album.md] In progressive rock the concept album is closely tied to the [[suite|multimovement suite]] and [[extended-composition|extended composition]], where a single theme or narrative is developed across an entire LP.

## Canonical examples

- **[[pink-floyd|Pink Floyd]] – *The Wall*** (1979): a double album rock opera following "Pink", a jaded rock star building a psychological wall of isolation; one of the best-known concept albums, with over 30 million copies sold.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-the-wall.md]
- **[[yes|Yes]] – *Close to the Edge*** (1972): a single 18-minute four-movement suite built around one theme, an extreme of the one-album, one-concept format.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-close-to-the-edge.md]
- **[[yes|Yes]] – *Tales from Topographic Oceans*** (1973): a double concept album.
- **[[genesis|Genesis]] – *The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway*** (1974): a concept double album about a character's surreal journey.
- **The Who – *Tommy*** (1969): popularly thought of as the first rock opera.
- **[[rush|Rush]] – *2112*** (1976): a side-long dystopian concept suite.

## Relation to side-long suites

The concept album and the [[suite|side-long suite]] are closely related: a concept album may be built from a single extended piece (as in *Close to the Edge* and *2112*), or from a sequence of shorter songs unified by a narrative or theme (as in *The Wall* and *The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway*). Both approaches foreground the album as a unified work rather than a collection of singles.
