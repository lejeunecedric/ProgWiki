# Extended Composition

**Extended composition** is the progressive-rock practice of growing songs into **side-long movements** — pieces of 15–25+ minutes that occupy an entire LP side — and, at its extreme, of building whole albums as single continuous works. It is the formal engine that pushed rock past the three-minute pop single, giving the music symphonic scale and room for slow development, contrast and climax.

## How it manifests in prog
Where the pop single is built for radio brevity, the extended composition is built for **unfolding**: a long piece typically assembles several contrasting sections, themes and moods into one continuous arc rather than a verse–chorus loop. It draws on the classical idea of the [[suite]] — an ordered set of linked movements — and on the [[concept-album]]'s unifying theme, but concentrates that ambition into a single track. The result is music that rewards sustained listening and live performance, and that showcases the band's [[virtuosity]] and arranging craft.

## Canonical examples
- **"Echoes"** (Pink Floyd, *Meddle*, 1971) — 23+ minutes, taking up the entire second side of the LP; assembled from disparate musical experiments into a seamless side-long piece. ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-echoes-song.md]
- **"Supper's Ready"** (Genesis, *Foxtrot*, 1972) — a 23-minute multi-section suite.
- **"Close to the Edge"** (Yes, *Close to the Edge*, 1972) — an 18-minute four-movement suite.
- **"Tubular Bells"** (Mike Oldfield, 1973) — an album-length instrumental built as a single extended work.
- **The Dark Side of the Moon** (Pink Floyd, 1973) — a concept album whose tracks flow continuously, limiting the extended instrumentals of earlier work in favour of a unified whole. ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-dark-side-of-the-moon.md]

## Relationship to related concepts
Extended composition is the structural sibling of the [[suite]] (a linked set of movements) and the [[concept-album]] (a thematically unified album). It is also the natural home of [[analogue-synthesizer-texture]], whose sustained pads and washes need long-form space to breathe, and of [[odd-time-signatures]], which thrive in through-composed sections. Bands like [[pink-floyd]], [[yes]], [[genesis]] and [[king-crimson]] made it a defining feature of classic prog.
