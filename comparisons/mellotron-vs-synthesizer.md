# Mellotron vs Synthesizer

The two keyboard sounds that define classic prog are often conflated, but they are opposites in principle: the [[mellotron|Mellotron]] is a **tape-based sampler** (it plays back recorded sounds), while the [[moog-synthesizer|Moog synthesizer]] is a **voltage-controlled generator** (it creates sounds from oscillators). Both arrived in the late 1960s and became the twin signatures of the genre.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-mellotron.md]^[raw/articles/wikipedia-moog-synthesizer.md]

## Dimensions of comparison

| Dimension | Mellotron (1963) | Moog synthesizer (1964) / Minimoog (1970) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Principle | Electro-mechanical: each key pulls a length of magnetic tape across a playback head | Electronic: voltage-controlled oscillators, filters and envelope generators (subtractive synthesis) |
| Sound source | Recorded samples — strings, choir, flute, brass | Synthesized waveforms (sawtooth, square, sine) shaped by filters |
| Character | Warm, organic, slightly unstable; the "string section in a box" | Clean, futuristic, precise; sweeping filter sweeps and lead tones |
| Playability | ~8-second tape loops per key; sustain limited by tape length | Fully sustained notes; pitch-bend and modulation wheels |
| Prog role | Texture and orchestral colour ([[king-crimson|King Crimson]], early [[genesis|Genesis]], [[yes|Yes]]) | Lead lines, bass, and atmospheric pads ([[emerson-lake-palmer|ELP]], [[rick-wakeman|Wakeman]], [[pink-floyd|Pink Floyd]]) |
| Reliability | Notoriously fragile (tapes stretch, capstans jam) | Robust, but early modulars were huge and expensive |
| Iconic users | [[robert-fripp|Fripp]], [[peter-gabriel|Gabriel]]'s Genesis, [[jon-anderson|Anderson]]'s Yes | [[keith-emerson|Emerson]], [[rick-wakeman|Wakeman]], Tangerine Dream |

## Verdict / synthesis
They are complementary, not competing: the Mellotron supplied the **organic, orchestral warmth** that made prog sound "classical", while the Moog supplied the **electronic future** that made it sound "progressive". The classic 1970s keyboard rig (Emerson, Wakeman) stacked both — a Mellotron for strings and choir, a Minimoog for leads and bass. The Minimoog in particular became a staple of 1970s progressive rock, used by Yes, Tangerine Dream and ELP.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-minimoog.md]
