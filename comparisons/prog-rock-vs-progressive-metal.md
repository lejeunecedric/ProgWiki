# Prog rock vs progressive metal

## Overview

[[progressive-metal|Progressive metal]] is a broad fusion genre melding heavy metal and [[progressive-rock|progressive rock]], combining the loud aggression and amplified, distorted guitar-driven sound of the former with the more experimental, cerebral, or quasi-classical compositions of the latter.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-prog-metal.md] Classic 1970s progressive rock and progressive metal share a great deal — virtuosity, odd metres, and concept albums — but differ in heaviness, guitar riffing, and production aggression.

## Comparison table

| Dimension | Classic prog rock (1970s) | Progressive metal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Era** | Mid-to-late 1960s through 1970s | Emerged late-1980s; widespread success in the 1990s |
| **Core sound** | Long solos, extended pieces, keyboards (Mellotron, Hammond, Moog), pastoral-to-symphonic textures | Loud, distorted, guitar-driven aggression with complex rhythms |
| **Virtuosity** | Obsessive dedication to technical skill and virtuosity | Extreme technical proficiency of performers |
| **Rhythm** | Odd time signatures, suites, extended composition | Frequent meter changes, intense syncopation, polymeters |
| **Concept albums** | Central — *Close to the Edge*, *The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway* | Shared — *Operation: Mindcrime*, *2112* |
| **Heaviness** | Moderate; rock instrumentation | High; amplified distorted guitar, metal aggression |
| **Key bands** | Yes, Genesis, King Crimson, Pink Floyd, ELP | Dream Theater, Queensrÿche, Fates Warning, Tool, Mastodon |

## Verdict / synthesis

Classic 1970s prog and progressive metal share the same DNA — virtuosity, odd metres, extended composition, and the concept album — but diverge on heaviness and production. Progressive metal takes the experimental, cerebral composition of prog and marries it to the loud, distorted, guitar-driven aggression of heavy metal, with extreme technical proficiency and intense syncopation.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-prog-metal.md] The bridge between the two is [[rush|Rush]], widely recognized as bridging hard rock, English progressive rock, and pure heavy metal: *2112* (1976) showcased technical expertise and complex composition while using a more direct, heavier approach than the established English prog sound.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-prog-metal.md] Modern bands such as [[tool|Tool]] and Dream Theater carried this mantle into the 1990s and beyond, making progressive metal the direct descendant of classic prog.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-prog-metal.md]
