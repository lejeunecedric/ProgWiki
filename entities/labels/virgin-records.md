# Virgin Records

**Virgin Records** was a British record label founded in 1972 by Richard Branson, Simon Draper, Nik Powell and musician Tom Newman, growing to become a major label alongside A&M and Island. ^[raw/articles/virgin-records.md]

## Founding

Branson and Powell had initially run a small record shop called Virgin Records and Tapes on Notting Hill Gate, London, specialising in krautrock imports. After making the shop a success they turned it into a fully fledged record label. The original Virgin logo (the "Gemini"/"Twins" logo) was designed by English artist and illustrator Roger Dean. ^[raw/articles/virgin-records.md]

## Tubular Bells and the launch

The first release on the label was Mike Oldfield's progressive rock album *Tubular Bells* (1973), for which the fledgling label garnered unprecedented acclaim. It was soon followed by notable krautrock releases, including Tangerine Dream's *Phaedra* (a Top 20 hit), *The Faust Tapes* and *Faust IV* by Faust, and Gong's *Flying Teapot* (Radio Gnome Invisible, Pt. 1). ^[raw/articles/virgin-records.md]

## The Manor studio

Virgin's progressive/ambient leaning was anchored by its own recording facility, the Manor, where early albums were produced. The label was initially one of the key labels of English and European progressive rock. ^[raw/articles/virgin-records.md]

## Why it mattered

Virgin gave a home to progressive, krautrock and ambient artists at a time when the majors were retreating from the genre. The 1977 signing of the Sex Pistols reinvented the label as a new wave outpost. The current Virgin "scrawl" logo was first used on Mike Oldfield's *Incantations* album (1978). ^[raw/articles/virgin-records.md]

## Later history

Virgin Records was sold by Branson to Thorn EMI in June 1992 for a reported US$1 billion. After EMI was taken over by Universal in 2012, Virgin became part of Universal and later an imprint of the rebranded EMI/Virgin EMI Records. ^[raw/articles/virgin-records.md]
