# Ian Anderson

**Ian Scott Anderson** (born 10 August 1947, Dunfermline, Scotland) is a British musician, best known as the flautist, singer, acoustic guitarist and leader of [[jethro-tull|Jethro Tull]].^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]

## Key facts
- Co-founded Jethro Tull; the band's name (after the 18th-century English agriculturalist) was supplied by a booking agent's history enthusiast and stuck.^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]
- On *This Was* (1968), Tull's debut, he had been playing flute only a few months; his self-taught fingering was later corrected after he noticed his daughter's flute lessons.^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]^[raw/articles/jethro-tull.md]
- Led Tull from blues rock (*This Was*, *Stand Up*) into folk-tinged symphonic prog culminating in the [[suite|single-track]] albums *Thick as a Brick* (1972) and *A Passion Play* (1973).^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]^[raw/articles/jethro-tull.md]
- The one-legged flute pose arose by accident — standing on one leg to balance while playing harmonica — and was misreported as a flute pose by a journalist; it became his signature.^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]
- Continued under his own name and Jethro Tull banners; released *Thick as a Brick 2* (2012), a sequel, and still performs prolifically.^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]

## Signature sound
Flute as a lead rock instrument is Anderson's core contribution — far more portable on the road than a guitar and distinctive as a front-line voice. He pairs it with vocal, acoustic guitar, harmonica and theatrical stage persona (the one-legged stance, later taking the whole band under a rock "roots and branches" folk aesthetic). His songwriting blends hard rock, English folk and classical influences into long-form conceptual pieces.^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]^[raw/articles/jethro-tull.md]

## Influence
Anderson showed prog that a band could drop conventional guitar-hero fronting altogether and let a woodwind carry the hooks; his folk-prog hybrid and theatrical conceptualism influenced later folk-prog and neo-prog bands.^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]
