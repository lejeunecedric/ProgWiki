# Kevin Ayers

**Kevin Ayers** (16 August 1944, Herne Bay, Kent – 18 February 2013, Montolieu, France) was an English singer-songwriter and bassist, a founding member of [[soft-machine|Soft Machine]] in the mid-1960s and a central figure of the [[canterbury-scene|Canterbury scene]]. Rock journalist Nick Kent wrote: "Kevin Ayers and [[syd-barrett|Syd Barrett]] were the two most important people in British pop music. Everything that came after came from them."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-kevin-ayers.md]

## Soft Machine and the Wilde Flowers

Ayers joined the Wilde Flowers — the Canterbury band that also included Robert Wyatt, Hugh Hopper and future members of Caravan — then left with Wyatt to form Soft Machine with Mike Ratledge and Daevid Allen. He switched to bass and shared vocals with Wyatt; the band shared stages with [[pink-floyd|Pink Floyd]] at the UFO Club, and its 1967 debut single "Love Makes Sweet Music" was among the first recordings of the British psychedelic movement. After a US tour opening for the Jimi Hendrix Experience he left the band and went to Ibiza with Allen.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-kevin-ayers.md]

## Solo career

His first album, *Joy of a Toy* (November 1969), was one of the first releases on the new [[harvest-records|Harvest]] label, alongside Pink Floyd's *Ummagumma*. With the Whole World (a young Mike Oldfield on bass, composer David Bedford on keyboards, saxophonist Lol Coxhill) he made *Shooting at the Moon* (October 1970), followed by *Whatevershebringswesing* (1971) — whose mellifluous eight-minute title track set his signature 1970s sound — and *Bananamour* (1973), with the tribute "Oh! Wot A Dream" to Syd Barrett. *The Confessions of Dr. Dream and Other Stories* (May 1974) marked a move to Island and the start of a twenty-year partnership with guitarist Ollie Halsall. He was, to all intents, a member of [[gong|Gong]] during their first UK tour in 1971, and helped bring Steve Hillage into the band.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-kevin-ayers.md]

After years of retreat in Deià, Mallorca, he returned to record his last album, *The Unfairground* (2007), and died in his sleep in France in 2013, aged 68.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-kevin-ayers.md]
