# Bill Bruford

**William Scott Bruford** (born 17 May 1949, Sevenoaks, Kent) is an English drummer and percussionist, known for his work from the late 1960s to the 1990s primarily as a founding member of [[yes|Yes]] and as a member of three incarnations of [[king-crimson|King Crimson]].^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md]

## Key facts
- Co-founded Yes in 1968 and recorded five albums with them, including *The Yes Album*, *Fragile* (both 1971), and *Close to the Edge* (1972).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md]
- Left Yes in 1972 to join King Crimson, later explaining: "King Crimson was one of the only gigs for a rock drummer where you could play in 17/16 and still stay in decent hotels".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md] In July 1972, after *Close to the Edge* had been recorded, Bruford quit to join the band.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md]
- Featured on *Larks' Tongues in Aspic* (1973), *Starless and Bible Black* (1974), *Red* (1974), and the live album *USA* (1975); Fripp disbanded King Crimson in September 1974.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md]
- Toured with Genesis in 1976 on their *A Trick of the Tail* tour as a temporary fill-in for Phil Collins on drums.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md]
- Returned to King Crimson for the 1981–84 Discipline-era line-up (with Fripp, Adrian Belew, and Tony Levin), recording *Discipline* (1981), *Beat* (1982), and *Three of a Perfect Pair* (1984).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md]
- Played a third and final King Crimson tenure from 1994 to 1997 as part of the "Double Trio", alongside Pat Mastelotto.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md]
- Retired from professional drumming in 2009; in 2016 he ranked No. 16 on *Rolling Stone*'s list of the "100 Greatest Drummers of All Time"; in 2017 he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Yes.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md]

## Musical approach
In Yes, Bruford's main interest was allowing the drums to "be heard", and so he developed a style that involved "unusual beat placement" and time signatures, learning "how to read the horizontal lines, but not the vertical notes".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md] He credited six months of free-jazz percussionist Jamie Muir's influence while in King Crimson as highly formative on him as a player.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md] His motivations for joining Crimson over staying with Yes were explicitly musical and exploratory.
