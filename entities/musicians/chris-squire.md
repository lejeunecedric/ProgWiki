# Chris Squire

## Overview

Chris Squire (4 March 1948 – 27 June 2015) was an English musician, singer and songwriter, best known as the bassist, backing vocalist, and only constant member of the progressive rock band [[yes|Yes]] until his death. He was widely regarded as the dominant bassist among the English progressive rock bands, influencing peers and later generations of bassists with his incisive sound and elaborately contoured, melodic bass lines.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chris-squire.md]

## Key facts

- Born 4 March 1948 in Kingsbury, London; died 27 June 2015 in Phoenix, Arizona.
- Purchased his first [[rickenbacker-4001-bass|Rickenbacker 4001]] in 1965 using a staff discount at a Boosey & Hawkes shop.
- The only member to play on each of the 21 studio albums Yes released from 1969 to 2014.
- Ranked the 18th greatest bass player of all time by *Rolling Stone* in 2020.

## Signature sound

Squire's name was associated with his trademark instrument, the Rickenbacker 4001 (British model RM1999). He popularized using roundwound strings on the 4001 to create a bright, twangy bass tone, a sound that inspired other progressive rock bassists including Geddy Lee of [[rush|Rush]].^[raw/articles/wikipedia-rickenbacker-4001.md] His melodic, lead-style bass lines and close vocal harmonies with [[jon-anderson|Jon Anderson]] and [[steve-howe|Steve Howe]] were central to Yes's [[symphonic-prog|symphonic progressive rock]] sound.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chris-squire.md]

## Role in Yes

Squire co-founded Yes with Anderson in 1968, and the two shared interests in Simon & Garfunkel and vocal harmonies. He co-wrote much of the band's music with Howe, and supplied backing vocals in harmony with Anderson on songs such as "South Side of the Sky" and "Close to the Edge". He described his playing on "The Remembering (High the Memory)" from *Tales from Topographic Oceans* (1973) as "one of the nicest things I think I've ever played".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chris-squire.md]
