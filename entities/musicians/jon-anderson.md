# Jon Anderson

## Overview

Jon Anderson (born John Roy Anderson, 25 October 1944) is an English singer, songwriter and musician who co-founded the progressive rock band [[yes|Yes]] with bassist [[chris-squire|Chris Squire]] in 1968 and rose to prominence as their lead vocalist. Known for his high tenor vocal style, Anderson was a member of Yes across three tenures: 1968 to 1980, 1983 to 1988, and 1990 to 2008.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-anderson.md]

## Key facts

- Born 25 October 1944 in Accrington, Lancashire, England.
- Co-founded Yes in 1968; first gig with the band on 3 August 1968.
- Became an American citizen in 2009.
- Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Yes in 2017.

## Role in Yes

Although the band had no formal leader, Anderson served as its main motivating force in their early days, doing most of the hustling for gigs and originating most of their songs. He played a key role in initiating the band's more ambitious artistic ideas, serving as the main instigator of songs including "Close to the Edge", "The Gates of Delirium", and "Awaken", and the concept behind the double [[concept-album|concept album]] *Tales from Topographic Oceans* (1973).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-anderson.md]

## Signature style

Anderson's high-register, spiritual tenor and optimistic, mystical lyric themes are central to Yes's sound. His vocal harmonies with Squire and Howe became a defining feature of the band's [[symphonic-prog|symphonic progressive rock]].^[raw/articles/wikipedia-yes-band.md]

## Solo and side projects

Anderson left Yes in 1980 and pursued a solo career begun with *Olias of Sunhillow* (1976), collaborating with Vangelis (as Jon and Vangelis), Roine Stolt, and the Anderson Bruford Wakeman Howe offshoot.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-jon-anderson.md]
