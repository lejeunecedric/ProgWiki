# Steve Howe

## Overview

Steve Howe (born 8 April 1947) is an English musician, best known as the guitarist and backing vocalist in the progressive rock band [[yes|Yes]] across three stints since 1970. Howe's blend of acoustic and electric guitar helped shape the sound and direction of the band, leading to more commercial and critical success. He is the band's longest-serving member.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-steve-howe.md]

## Key facts

- Born 8 April 1947 in Holloway, north London.
- Joined Yes in June 1970, replacing Peter Banks; first album with them was *The Yes Album* (1971).
- Co-wrote "Yours Is No Disgrace", "Starship Trooper", and "Roundabout".
- Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Yes in 2017.

## Signature style

Howe's proficiency with a wide range of guitars — electric, acoustic, lap steel, and more — made him a prolific and essential part of Yes's change in musical direction towards [[symphonic-prog|progressive rock]]. *The Yes Album* and *Fragile* include his solo acoustic pieces "Clap" and "Mood for a Day". He added a unique approach to lap steel guitar on *Close to the Edge* (1972).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-steve-howe.md] His jazz-rock phrasing and multi-instrumental acoustic work set him apart from the harder-rocking guitarists of the era.

## Solo and side projects

Howe built a prolific solo career (20 solo albums) and worked with Asia, GTR, and Anderson Bruford Wakeman Howe alongside [[jon-anderson|Jon Anderson]], [[bill-bruford|Bill Bruford]] and [[rick-wakeman|Rick Wakeman]].^[raw/articles/wikipedia-steve-howe.md]
