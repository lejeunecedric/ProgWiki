# Greg Lake

**Gregory Stuart Lake** (10 November 1947 – 7 December 2016) was an English musician, singer, songwriter and bassist, best known as the voice and bassist of [[emerson-lake-palmer|Emerson, Lake & Palmer]] and as the original vocalist/bassist of [[king-crimson|King Crimson]].^[raw/articles/greg-lake.md]

## Key facts
- Raised in Dorset alongside [[robert-fripp|Robert Fripp]]; he and Fripp took lessons with the same guitar teacher (Don S. G. Terry).^[raw/articles/greg-lake.md]
- In 1969, at [[robert-fripp|Fripp]]'s invitation, joined King Crimson as lead singer and bassist, contributing lyrics to their debut *In the Court of the Crimson King*; he left after the album's release.^[raw/articles/greg-lake.md]
- Formed ELP in 1970 with [[keith-emerson|Keith Emerson]] and [[carl-palmer|Carl Palmer]], writing or co-writing signature tracks including "Lucky Man" and "From the Beginning".^[raw/articles/greg-lake.md]^[raw/articles/emerson-lake-palmer.md]
- Briefly joined the 1980s supergroup Asia in 1983, replacing vocalist/bassist John Wetton (another King Crimson alumnus) for concerts in Tokyo, including the "Asia in Asia" show at the Nippon Budokan on 6 December 1983.^[raw/articles/greg-lake.md]
- Died of cancer on 7 December 2016, aged 69.^[raw/articles/greg-lake.md]

## Signature sound
Lake's powerful, expressive singing and melodic bass playing anchored both King Crimson's debut and ELP's symphonic sound, equally at home on the gentle acoustic "Lucky Man" and over ELP's heavy orchestral climaxes. He had primarily been a guitarist before joining King Crimson, where he first played bass guitar; with ELP he began on a Fender bass before switching to a Gibson Ripper.^[raw/articles/greg-lake.md]^[raw/articles/emerson-lake-palmer.md]

## Influence
Lake's voice became a benchmark for the prog singer archetype — melodic, dynamic, folk-tinged — and his bass playing was a through-line from Crimson's early sound into ELP's symphonic rock.^[raw/articles/greg-lake.md]
