# John Wetton

**John Wetton** (John Kenneth Wetton, 12 June 1949 – 31 January 2017) was an English musician, singer, and songwriter, known for his bass playing and his baritone voice. He is best remembered in prog for his tenure as bassist and lead vocalist of [[king-crimson|King Crimson]] (1972–74) and as a co-founder of the supergroup Asia.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-john-wetton.md]

## Key facts
- Born in Willington, Derbyshire, and grew up in Bournemouth, Dorset; his elder brother Robert was a classical organist and choirmaster, and John credited Bach bass lines with sparking his love of the bass.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-john-wetton.md]
- Played in a number of bands with Richard Palmer-James (including the Corvettes, Tetrad, and Ginger Man) before joining Family in 1971.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-john-wetton.md]
- In late 1972 Fripp invited him to join King Crimson, alongside violinist David Cross, drummer [[bill-bruford|Bill Bruford]], and percussionist Jamie Muir; his friend Palmer-James became the band's primary lyricist.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-john-wetton.md]
- Remained with King Crimson until Fripp disbanded the group in 1974, appearing on *Larks' Tongues in Aspic* (1973), *Starless and Bible Black* (1974), and *Red* (1974).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-john-wetton.md]
- After Crimson he worked with Roxy Music (1974–75), Uriah Heep (1975–76), and in 1977 formed U.K. with Bruford, Eddie Jobson, and Allan Holdsworth.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-john-wetton.md]
- In late 1981 co-founded Asia with Steve Howe, Geoff Downes, and Carl Palmer; the self-titled debut (1982) sold over ten million copies and became *Billboard*'s number one album of 1982.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-john-wetton.md]
- Died of complications of colorectal cancer on 31 January 2017, aged 67.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-john-wetton.md]

## Role in King Crimson
Wetton was the band's strongest singer/bassist since Greg Lake's departure, per AllMusic's retrospective on *Larks' Tongues in Aspic*.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-larks-tongues-in-aspic.md] He gained experience as lead singer and songwriter during his Crimson years, and his baritone voice and melodic bass anchored the heavy, improvisational 1972–74 sound.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-john-wetton.md]

## Influence
Artists citing Wetton as an influence include Billy Sheehan, Michael Sweet of Stryper, and Kevin Feazey of the Fierce and the Dead; after his death Eric Clapton published a short instrumental tribute, "For John W."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-john-wetton.md]
