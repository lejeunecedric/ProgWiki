# Robert Wyatt

**Robert Wyatt** (born Robert Wyatt-Ellidge, 28 January 1945, Bristol) is an English retired musician, a founding member of the Canterbury scene bands [[soft-machine|Soft Machine]] and Matching Mole. He began as a kit drummer and singer, became paraplegic after falling from a fourth-floor window on 1 June 1973 at a party in Maida Vale, and used the injury as the pivot to a forty-year solo career on keyboards, trumpet and voice. He retired from music in 2014, saying "there is a pride in [stopping], I don't want [the music] to go off."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-robert-wyatt.md]

## Wilde Flowers, Soft Machine, Matching Mole

Wyatt grew up in Lydden, Kent, took drum lessons from American jazz drummer George Neidorf, and befriended expatriate Australian musician Daevid Allen, who lodged with the family. After a spell in Mallorca and the Daevid Allen Trio (with Hugh Hopper), he co-founded the Wilde Flowers with [[kevin-ayers|Kevin Ayers]], Richard Sinclair and Brian Hopper — drumming, then singing after Ayers left. When the Wilde Flowers split in 1966 into [[caravan|Caravan]] and Soft Machine, Wyatt joined Soft Machine with Ratledge, drumming and sharing vocals with Ayers. Unhappy that his musical ideas were being rejected, he left in 1971 after three albums, played briefly in the fusion bigband Centipede, and formed **Matching Mole** ("machine molle" — a pun on Soft Machine), a largely instrumental outfit that cut two albums before his accident ended it.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-robert-wyatt.md]

## Solo career

On 4 November 1973 [[pink-floyd|Pink Floyd]] played two benefit concerts at London's Rainbow Theatre (supported by Soft Machine, compered by John Peel) that raised a reported £10,000 for him. His solo album *Rock Bottom* — largely composed before the accident and rearranged during convalescence, with Mike Oldfield, Ivor Cutler and [[henry-cow|Henry Cow]]'s Fred Frith — was released on 26 July 1974, produced by Floyd drummer Nick Mason, as was his next single, a cover of the Monkees' "I'm a Believer" that reached No. 29 in the UK. *Ruth Is Stranger Than Richard* (1975) was more jazz-led, with Brian Eno guesting. Throughout the 1970s he guested with [[henry-cow|Henry Cow]] (on *Concerts*), [[hatfield-and-the-north|Hatfield and the North]], Carla Bley, Eno and Phil Manzanera.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-robert-wyatt.md]

His work became increasingly politicised from the early 1980s: a Communist Party of Great Britain member, his original version of "Shipbuilding" (Elvis Costello/Clive Langer, Falklands-inspired) reached No. 35 in 1983, and *Old Rottenhat* (1985) collected sparse, strongly political songs played mostly alone. Later albums include *Dondestan* (1991), *Shleep* (1997) and the Mercury-nominated *Cuckooland* (2003); in 2001 he curated the Meltdown festival and sang "Comfortably Numb" with David Gilmour. He is married to painter and songwriter Alfreda Benge, his longtime collaborator.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-robert-wyatt.md]
