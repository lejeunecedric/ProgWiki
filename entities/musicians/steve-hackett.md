# Steve Hackett

**Stephen Richard Hackett** (born 12 February 1950) is an English guitarist who gained prominence as the lead guitarist of [[genesis|Genesis]] from 1971 to 1977, and who later built a substantial solo career. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Genesis in 2010. ^[raw/articles/steve-hackett.md]

## Key facts
- Joined Genesis in 1971 after answering a *Melody Maker* advertisement that was responded to by [[peter-gabriel|Peter Gabriel]]; his first recording with the band was *Nursery Cryme* (1971).^[raw/articles/steve-hackett.md]
- Contributed to six Genesis studio albums, three live albums, seven singles and one EP before leaving in 1977.^[raw/articles/steve-hackett.md]
- Featured prominently on solos for "The Musical Box", "The Return of the Giant Hogweed" and "The Fountain of Salmacis".^[raw/articles/steve-hackett.md]
- Composed "Horizons" on *Foxtrot* (1972), based on a Bach cello suite; "Firth of Fifth" on *Selling England by the Pound* contains one of his most famous solos.^[raw/articles/steve-hackett.md]
- Co-founded the supergroup GTR with [[steve-howe|Steve Howe]] in 1986.^[raw/articles/steve-hackett.md]

## Signature sound
Hackett was an early proponent of two-handed guitar tapping (a technique usually attributed to Eddie Van Halen) and of sweep picking, and was known for fingerpicking and classical-guitar textures. He was the only member of Genesis during his tenure who did not sing.^[raw/articles/steve-hackett.md]

## History
Hackett left Genesis in 1977 to pursue a solo career, releasing a large body of work spanning progressive rock, blues, world music and classical styles. His solo output includes *Voyage of the Acolyte* (1975) and many later albums.^[raw/articles/steve-hackett.md]

## Influence & legacy
Hackett's tapping and sweep-picking techniques influenced guitarists including Eddie Van Halen, Brian May, Alex Lifeson and Steve Rothery, and his classical-guitar approach became a signature of the Genesis sound.^[raw/articles/steve-hackett.md]
