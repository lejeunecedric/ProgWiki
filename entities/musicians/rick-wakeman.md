# Rick Wakeman

## Overview

Rick Wakeman (born 18 May 1949) is an English keyboardist and composer best known for his multiple tenures in the progressive rock band [[yes|Yes]] and for his prolific solo career spanning six decades. His most successful and acclaimed albums are his first three progressive rock [[concept-album|concept albums]] — *The Six Wives of Henry VIII* (1973), the UK number-one *Journey to the Centre of the Earth* (1974), and *The Myths and Legends of King Arthur and the Knights of the Round Table* (1975).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-rick-wakeman.md]

## Key facts

- Born 18 May 1949 in Perivale, Middlesex, England.
- Quit the Royal College of Music in 1969 to become a session musician.
- Played on "Space Oddity" and "Life on Mars?" for David Bowie, and "Morning Has Broken" by Cat Stevens.
- Joined Yes in 1971; completed five stints with the band until 2004.
- Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Yes in 2017; appointed CBE in 2021.

## Signature style

Wakeman's technical virtuosity on the [[hammond-organ|Hammond organ]], [[moog-synthesizer|Moog synthesizer]] and [[mellotron|Mellotron]], combined with his flamboyant stage presence and trademark capes, made him an iconic figure in 1970s progressive rock.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-rick-wakeman.md] On *Close to the Edge* he played the pipe organ at St Giles-without-Cripplegate church in the Barbican, London, contributing the florid, classically inflected keyboard textures that defined Yes's [[symphonic-prog|symphonic sound]].^[raw/articles/wikipedia-close-to-the-edge.md]

## Solo career

Wakeman's solo work is notable for its orchestral, narrative concept albums and its vast productivity, with a discography exceeding 100 solo albums. His *Journey to the Centre of the Earth* reached No. 1 in the UK.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-rick-wakeman.md]
