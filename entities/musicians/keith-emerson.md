# Keith Emerson

**Keith Noel Emerson** (2 November 1944 – 11 March 2016) was an English keyboardist, songwriter, composer and record producer, one of prog's defining keyboard virtuosos. He played in the Nice before co-founding [[emerson-lake-palmer|Emerson, Lake & Palmer]] (ELP).^[raw/articles/keith-emerson.md]

## Key facts
- Born in Todmorden, Yorkshire; studied classical piano as a child.^[raw/articles/keith-emerson.md]
- Discovered the Hammond organ after hearing jazz organist Jack McDuff's "Rock Candy" in the late 1960s; his first Hammond was an L-100, later adding a C-3. He strongly preferred the C-3's sound ("far superior" to the cheaper L-100), and used the L-100 for stage stunts, throwing it around and making it feed back.^[raw/articles/keith-emerson.md]
- As leader of the Nice (late 1960s) he pioneered aggressive, theatrical abuse of the Hammond — including playing it with knives.^[raw/articles/keith-emerson.md]^[raw/articles/emerson-lake-palmer.md]
- ELP's record deal funded his own Moog modular synthesizer (a preset model patched by Eddie Vickers with six distinctive sounds that became "the foundation of ELP's sound"); Emerson was the first artist to tour with a Moog.^[raw/articles/keith-emerson.md]
- Died by suicide on 11 March 2016 in Santa Monica, California; he had suffered nerve damage since around 1993 that hampered his playing and fed his anxieties about performing.^[raw/articles/keith-emerson.md]

## Signature sound & stagecraft
Emerson's signature was the [[hammond-organ|Hammond]] / [[analogue-synthesizer-texture|Moog]] rock-organ school he basically founded: two organs side by side facing the audience, drawbar-heavy tonewheel tone, feedback, sustain-solo playing, and theatrical organ abuse (knives, beating the instrument, feeding it back through the Leslie speakers). His [[classical-and-jazz-crossover|classical-rock fusion]] — notably his own keyboard takes on Mussorgsky's *Pictures at an Exhibition*, Bach, Janáček and Prokofiev — became a hallmark of ELP.^[raw/articles/keith-emerson.md]^[raw/articles/emerson-lake-palmer.md]

## Influence
Emerson blended jazz-organ agility with baroque and romantic classical rigour, defining keyboard virtuosity in [[symphonic-prog|symphonic prog]]; his Moog use helped drive the development of the synthesizer as a prog lead instrument. He headlined both the first Moogfest and the third, and was honoured (with Moog) at the Smithsonian's "The Keyboard Meets Modern Technology".^[raw/articles/keith-emerson.md]
