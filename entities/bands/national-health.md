# National Health

**National Health** were an English progressive rock band associated with the [[canterbury-scene|Canterbury scene]], founded in 1975 from members of Dave Stewart's [[hatfield-and-the-north|Hatfield and the North]] and Alan Gowen's Gilgamesh. Original members included guitarists Phil Miller and Phil Lee and bassist Mont Campbell; [[bill-bruford|Bill Bruford]] (previously of [[yes|Yes]] and [[king-crimson|King Crimson]]) was the initial drummer, soon replaced by Pip Pyle. The band was named after Stewart's National Health Service spectacles.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-national-health.md]

## History

- Campbell was replaced by Neil Murray and then John Greaves; keyboardist Alan Gowen left before the first album but returned for the final tours, replacing Dave Stewart (who resigned after the second album). Guitarist Phil Miller was the band's only constant member.
- They toured extensively and released *National Health* (1978) — created during the rise of punk rock, but characterized by lengthy, mostly instrumental compositions — and *Of Queues and Cures* (1978). They performed live until winter 1980.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-national-health.md]
- After Gowen's death in May 1981, the *Queues* lineup (Stewart, Miller, Greaves, Pyle) reunited to record *D.S. Al Coda* (1982), a set of Gowen compositions, most previously unrecorded.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-national-health.md]
- Later personnel connections touch [[henry-cow|Henry Cow]]: bassoonist Lindsay Cooper and cellist Georgie Born appear in the 1978 line-ups, and Elton Dean guested with the 1981–83 reunion.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-national-health.md]

## Sound

National Health carried the Canterbury scene's most compositionally intricate, jazz-inflected wing into the late 1970s: long instrumental pieces with interlocking keyboards (Stewart, Gowen), angular guitar (Miller) and odd-metre ensemble writing — kin to [[soft-machine|Soft Machine]] and [[gentle-giant|Gentle Giant]] rather than to vocal-led symphonic prog.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-national-health.md]
