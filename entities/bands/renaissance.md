# Renaissance

**Renaissance** are an English progressive rock band formed in 1969, among the few prog groups who leaned fully into Western classical material rather than just classical *flavour*.^[raw/articles/progressive-rock.md]

## Key facts
- One of the major British prog bands whose debut appeared in the founding wave of 1968-1970, alongside [[jethro-tull|Jethro Tull]], [[king-crimson|King Crimson]], [[yes|Yes]], [[genesis|Genesis]], [[van-der-graaf-generator|Van der Graaf Generator]] and [[gentle-giant|Gentle Giant]].^[raw/articles/progressive-rock.md]
- Listed with [[emerson-lake-palmer|ELP]] and Ekseption as the small minority who incorporated literal classical themes to a significant degree.^[raw/articles/symphonic-prog.md]
- Reached maturity in the early 1970s wave of ambitious concept albums and side-long compositions that defined [[symphonic-prog|symphonic prog]].^[raw/articles/symphonic-prog.md]
- Singer Annie Haslam is the notable female exception in a genre that was almost entirely male.^[raw/articles/progressive-rock.md]
- [[john-wetton|John Wetton]] played live with the band early in his career and in 2013 guested on their album *Grandine il vento* — as did [[ian-anderson|Ian Anderson]], playing flute on "Cry to the World".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-john-wetton.md] ^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]

## Why they matter
Renaissance is the classical-crossover extreme of [[symphonic-prog|symphonic prog]]: orchestral arrangements, Haslam's operatic voice and long suites — a template for the genre's later chamber-prog descendants, and a living example of [[classical-and-jazz-crossover|classical crossover]].
