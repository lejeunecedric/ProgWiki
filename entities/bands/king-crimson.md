# King Crimson

**King Crimson** were an English progressive rock band formed in London in 1968, led by guitarist and primary composer [[robert-fripp|Robert Fripp]] — the group's sole constant member throughout its history. Widely regarded as a foundational catalyst of the progressive rock movement, the band drew on classical, jazz, folk, heavy metal, gamelan, post-punk, industrial, and experimental music, and exerted a lasting influence on later alternative rock, metal, and math rock. ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md]

## Key facts / history
- Formed 30 November 1968 by Fripp, Michael Giles, Greg Lake, Ian McDonald, and lyricist Peter Sinfield; rehearsals began 13 January 1969.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md]
- Debut *In the Court of the Crimson King* (October 1969, Island) was an immediate critical and commercial success and is widely cited as a definitive cornerstone of progressive rock; Pete Townshend called it "an uncanny masterpiece".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md]
- Original line-up dissolved after the US tour (last show 14 December 1969); the band then passed through *In the Wake of Poseidon* (1970), *Lizard* (1970), and *Islands* (1971) with unstable personnel.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md]
- In 1972 Fripp rebuilt the band around [[bill-bruford|Bill Bruford]], [[john-wetton|John Wetton]], David Cross, and Jamie Muir, leaning into European free improvisation and avant-garde classical structures; the line-up stripped to a core trio of Fripp, Bruford, and Wetton for *Larks' Tongues in Aspic* (1973), *Starless and Bible Black* (1974), and *Red* (1974).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md]
- Returned in 1981 after a seven-year hiatus with Fripp, Bruford, and Americans Adrian Belew and Tony Levin, recording *Discipline* (1981), *Beat* (1982), and *Three of a Perfect Pair* (1984).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md]
- Reconstituted in 1994 as a "Double Trio" sextet (adding Pat Mastelotto and Trey Gunn) for *THRAK* (1995); the late-1990s improvised sub-groups were known as "ProjeKcts"; the early-2000s "Double Duo" released *The ConstruKction of Light* (2000) and *The Power to Believe* (2003).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md]
- Re-emerged in 2013 as a septet (later octet) with a three-drummer frontline and vocalist Jakko Jakszyk, performing rearranged material across the discography; the band ceased activity at the end of their December 2021 Japanese tour, with Fripp announcing it had "moved from sound to silence".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md]

## Signature sound
The early Crimson sound was built on the [[mellotron|Mellotron]] (suggested by Ian McDonald, inspired by the Moody Blues) over gothic, doom-laden arrangements and Sinfield's esoteric lyrics. Sinfield described the ethos: "If it sounded at all popular, it was out... If it sounded, like, too simple, we'd make it more complicated, we'd play it in 7/8 or 5/8, just to show off."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md] The 1972–74 incarnation replaced woodwinds with David Cross's violin and added free-improv percussion, producing aggressive, jazz-fusion and heavy-metal-adjacent passages.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-larks-tongues-in-aspic.md] The 1981 line-up pursued a "rock gamelan" of interlocking, picked guitar arpeggios (Fripp) and Belew's animal-like guitar sounds, with Levin on the [[chapman-stick|Chapman Stick]] and Bruford on electronic drums.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md]

## Key albums
- *In the Court of the Crimson King* (1969) — cornerstone debut; opens with "21st Century Schizoid Man", a proto-metal statement of purpose.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-21st-century-schizoid-man.md]
- *Larks' Tongues in Aspic* (1973) — the free-improv, avant-garde-metal pivot.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-larks-tongues-in-aspic.md]
- *Red* (1974) — the heavy trio-era climax.
- *Discipline* (1981) — the gamelan-influenced, post-progressive reinvention.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-discipline-album.md]

## Influence
King Crimson shaped contemporaries such as [[yes|Yes]] and [[genesis|Genesis]], and their innovations influenced later alternative rock, metal, and math rock. "21st Century Schizoid Man" is considered an influence on the later development of [[progressive-metal|progressive metal]] and industrial music.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-21st-century-schizoid-man.md]
