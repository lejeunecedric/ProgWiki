# Genesis

**Genesis** were an English rock band formed in 1967 at Charterhouse School in Godalming, Surrey, and one of the defining acts of [[symphonic-prog|symphonic progressive rock]]. Their career spans two sharply distinct eras: the theatrical, [[concept-album|concept-album]]-driven Gabriel years (1970–1975) and the commercially dominant Collins-led pop-prog years (1976–1991). ^[raw/articles/genesis-band.md]

## Key facts
- Formed 1967 at Charterhouse School by school pupils Peter Gabriel, [[tony-banks|Tony Banks]], Anthony Phillips, Mike Rutherford and drummer Chris Stewart; named by alumnus Jonathan King, who produced their debut *From Genesis to Revelation* (1969).^[raw/articles/genesis-band.md]
- Signed to [[charisma-records|Charisma Records]] in 1970 after label owner Tony Stratton Smith saw them at Ronnie Scott's; *Trespass* (1970) blended folk and progressive rock with time-signature changes.^[raw/articles/genesis-band.md]
- The classic six-man line-up — Gabriel, Banks, Rutherford, [[phil-collins|Phil Collins]] and [[steve-hackett|Steve Hackett]] — recorded *Nursery Cryme* (1971), *Foxtrot* (1972), *Selling England by the Pound* (1973) and the double concept album *The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway* (1974).^[raw/articles/genesis-band.md]
- Gabriel left in 1975; Collins took over lead vocals and, as a four-piece, the band released *A Trick of the Tail* and *Wind & Wuthering* (both 1976).^[raw/articles/genesis-band.md]
- Hackett left in 1977, leaving the trio of Banks, Rutherford and Collins; *...And Then There Were Three...* (1978) yielded their first major hit, "Follow You Follow Me".^[raw/articles/genesis-band.md]
- The pop-prog run — *Duke* (1980), *Abacab* (1981), *Genesis* (1983), *Invisible Touch* (1986) and *We Can't Dance* (1991) — brought massive commercial success, including the hit "No Son of Mine".^[raw/articles/genesis-band.md]
- Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2010; 100–150 million albums sold worldwide.^[raw/articles/genesis-band.md]

## Signature sound
The Gabriel-era sound paired Banks's [[mellotron|Mellotron]] and keyboards with Hackett's fingerpicked and tapped 12-string and electric guitar, Rutherford's bass and Collins's drums, over Gabriel's theatrical, story-driven vocals. The band were pioneers of the [[suite|extended suite]] and [[concept-album|concept album]], with [[odd-time-signatures|odd time signatures]] and folk-inflected pastoral passages.^[raw/articles/genesis-band.md]^[raw/articles/steve-hackett.md]

## History
After the commercial failure of their Decca debut, Genesis regrouped, signed to [[charisma-records|Charisma]] and built a live reputation on Gabriel's theatrical costumes and stage personae. The 1970s albums are considered progressive rock classics. Following Gabriel's 1975 departure, Collins fronted the band through its most commercially successful era, shifting from progressive rock into mainstream pop rock.^[raw/articles/genesis-band.md]^[raw/articles/peter-gabriel.md]

## Influence & legacy
Genesis are a keystone of [[symphonic-prog]] and a primary influence on the 1980s [[neo-prog]] revival (Marillion, IQ, Pendragon). Their two eras are frequently compared against [[yes|Yes]] and other classic-prog peers.^[raw/articles/neo-prog.md]
