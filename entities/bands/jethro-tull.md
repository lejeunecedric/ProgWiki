# Jethro Tull

**Jethro Tull** are an English rock band formed in 1967–68 around flautist, singer and songwriter [[ian-anderson|Ian Anderson]]. Beginning in the blues scene, they developed a distinctive progressive/folk-prog sound blending hard rock, English folk music and classical music; their flute-led, [[concept-album|concept-album]]-oriented albums made them one of classic prog's most singular acts.^[raw/articles/jethro-tull.md]

## Key facts
- Debut album *This Was* released October 1968, reaching No. 10 in the UK; original guitarist Mick Abrahams left shortly after, the key early line-up change.^[raw/articles/jethro-tull.md]
- Folk-influenced second album *Stand Up* (1969) reached No. 1 in the UK.^[raw/articles/jethro-tull.md]
- *Aqualung* (1971) — their best-known album; *Thick as a Brick* (1972), a single long composition conceived partly as parody of the concept album, fully established their progressive identity.^[raw/articles/jethro-tull.md]^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]
- *A Passion Play* (1973) continued the one-track [[suite|extended suite]] approach.^[raw/articles/jethro-tull.md]
- Approximately 60 million albums sold worldwide, 11 gold and 5 platinum certifications.^[raw/articles/jethro-tull.md]
- Named after the 18th-century English agriculturist Jethro Tull; the name stuck when the manager of the Marquee Club liked it over the earlier pseudonyms the booking agent had supplied.^[raw/articles/jethro-tull.md]

## Signature sound
Anderson's signature is the flute — a rare lead instrument in rock — plus electric guitar, harmonica and vocals, played with a distinctive one-legged stage pose and theatrical persona. The band moved from blues to a folk-rock-flavoured symphonic prog built on lengthy [[suite|suites]] and recurring conceptual narratives.^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]

## History
Formed by Anderson and schoolmates, Anderson's early bands' names were often supplied by booking agents' staff; Jethro Tull was one such name that stuck. Anderson initially played guitar and harmonica, switching to flute (which he had been playing only a few months when *This Was* was recorded) after seeing the band's live needs and, per his recounting, an incident that made him favour the portability of the flute on the road.^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]

## Influence & legacy
Alongside the strictly symphonic bands, Jethro Tull carved out a folk-prog lane that influenced later prog and folk-rock. Anderson's later solo work revisited the *Thick as a Brick* world (e.g. *Thick as a Brick 2*, 2012).^[raw/articles/ian-anderson.md]
