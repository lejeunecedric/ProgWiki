# Gong

**Gong** are a rock band formed in 1967 in Paris by Australian Daevid Allen and Gilli Smyth, associated with the [[canterbury-scene|Canterbury scene]] and mixing psychedelic, jazz and [[space-rock|space rock]] elements.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]

## Key facts
- Grew out of the Daevid Allen Trio lineage that also produced [[soft-machine|Soft Machine]].^[raw/articles/jazz-fusion.md]
- The 1973-74 "Radio Gnome Invisible" trilogy — the adventures of Zero the Hero, the Good Witch Yoni and the Pot Head Pixies from the Planet Gong — established the mythology of [[space-rock|space rock]].^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]
- *Flying Teapot* (Radio Gnome Invisible, Pt. 1) was released on [[virgin-records|Virgin]] amid the label's early krautrock and prog run.^[raw/articles/virgin-records.md]
- Reed player Didier Malherbe's description of the Canterbury aesthetic — "certain chord changes… a way of improvising that is very different from what is done in jazz" — is one of the scene's standard definitions.^[raw/articles/canterbury-scene.md]
- [[bill-bruford|Bill Bruford]] became a temporary member in late 1974 for a European tour after drummer Laurie Allan was busted for drugs at a border.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md]
- Punk-era offshoot Here & Now (who toured with Alternative TV) carried the Gong lineage into the post-punk underground.^[raw/articles/progressive-rock.md]

## Why they matter
Gong is the whimsical, mythological wing of [[canterbury-scene|Canterbury]]-adjacent prog: jazz-rock chops plus cosmic comedy, and the prototype of [[space-rock|space rock]] worldbuilding before Hawkwind mainstreamed the style.
