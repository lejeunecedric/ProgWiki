# Camel

English progressive rock band formed in **Guildford, Surrey, in 1971** by guitarist **Andrew Latimer**, drummer Andy Ward and bassist Doug Ferguson, who recruited keyboardist Peter Bardens. Originating from the blues-rock trio Brew, they made their live debut under the Camel name in London on 4 December 1971 and built a reputation as a proficient live ensemble blending progressive rock and jazz rock. ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-camel-band.md]

## Key facts & dates
- **1971** — Formed from the trio Brew; Bardens joins on keyboards; live debut in London.
- **1974** — *Mirage* — the band's breakthrough conceptual/thematic release.
- **1975** — *The Snow Goose* — an instrumental concept album and the band's critical breakthrough.
- **1976** — *Moonmadness*.
- **1977 / 1981** — *Rain Dances* and *Nude* mark a more commercial, jazz-oriented direction.
- **1984** — Band temporarily dissolves; Latimer later founds the independent label Camel Productions and reforms the band in **1991**.

## Signature sound
Camel are the archetype of **melodic symphonic prog**: Latimer's clean, lyrical guitar and flute-led textures over Bardens' keyboards, with a strong instrumental emphasis (the band lacked a dedicated lead vocalist and leaned on largely instrumental repertoire). Their sound is warmer and more pastoral than the harder-edged [[van-der-graaf-generator]] or [[king-crimson]], sitting squarely in the [[symphonic-prog]] mainstream alongside [[yes]] and [[genesis]]. The *Snow Goose* — an instrumental concept album — is their defining statement of seamless, atmospheric composition. ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-camel-band.md]

## Influence
Camel's music is widely cited as a primary influence on a later generation of progressive rock and metal artists, including Marillion, Opeth and Steven Wilson — a key bridge from classic prog to [[neo-prog]] and [[progressive-metal]]. Their melodic, flute-and-guitar instrumental writing also connects to the [[canterbury-scene]] sensibility.
