# Gentle Giant

British progressive rock band active **1970–1980**, known for the complexity and sophistication of its music and for the extraordinary versatility of its members — **all of the band were multi-instrumentalists**. Though never commercially successful, they achieved a dedicated cult following and were recognised with a lifetime achievement award at the 2015 Progressive Music Awards. ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-gentle-giant.md]

## Origins
The three Shulman brothers (Phil, Derek, Ray) — sons of a jazz trumpeter who pushed them to learn instruments from childhood — evolved out of Simon Dupree and the Big Sound, whose 1967 UK Top 10 hit "Kites" landed before the group committed to demanding progressive rock. The band's stated aim was to "expand the frontiers of contemporary popular music at the risk of becoming very unpopular." ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-gentle-giant.md]

## Signature sound
Gentle Giant's music was considered **complex even by progressive rock standards**, drawing on folk, soul, jazz and classical music — and, unlike most contemporaries, their classical influences spanned **medieval, baroque and modernist chamber music** rather than just the Romantic. The defining hallmarks are **intricate counterpoint, abrupt tempo/key shifts, and dense layered vocals** across all instruments — musicianship itself as the band's trademark, with albums like *Three Friends* (1972), *Octopus* (1972) and *The Power and the Glory* (1974) standing as landmarks. Despite erratic genre-political shifts over the decade, the ensemble's craft remained the constant.

## Influence
Gentle Giant pushed the complexity/frontier of what symphonic prog could hold, and are a touchstone for the more extreme, contrapuntal end of the genre along with [[king-crimson]] and [[van-der-graaf-generator]]. Their uncompromising multi-part writing is frequently cited by later [[progressive-metal]] and RIO ([[rock-in-opposition]]) acts.
