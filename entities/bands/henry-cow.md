# Henry Cow

**Henry Cow** were an English experimental rock group, founded at the University of Cambridge in **1968** by multi-instrumentalists Fred Frith and Tim Hodgkinson. Active 1968–1978, they were the founding band and organisational engine of [[rock-in-opposition|Rock in Opposition]]. Their personnel fluctuated constantly, but drummer Chris Cutler (from 1971), bassist John Greaves (1969–1976), bassoonist/oboist Lindsay Cooper and vocalist Dagmar Krause were key long-term members.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-henry-cow.md]

## Overview

An inherent anti-commercial attitude kept the band at arm's length from the music business and let them experiment at will: they worked as a collective in which the composer no longer owned a composition once the band worked on it, and they deliberately wrote music that challenged their own playing abilities. Early on, resident Cambridge composer Roger Smalley exposed them to [[soft-machine|Soft Machine]], Captain Beefheart and Frank Zappa and to the idea of writing long, complex pieces for rock groups. One of their first concerts was supporting [[pink-floyd|Pink Floyd]] at Cambridge's Architects' Ball, 12 June 1968. Despite the widespread assumption, band members repeatedly denied the name referenced composer Henry Cowell.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-henry-cow.md]

## Recordings

Signed to [[virgin-records|Virgin]], they recorded *Legend* (1973, at The Manor) and *Unrest* (1974), then merged briefly with Slapp Happy for *Desperate Straights* (1975) and *In Praise of Learning* (1975), which brought Krause into the band. Their last album, the instrumental *Western Culture*, was recorded at Sunrise Studios, Switzerland in August 1978 and released on their own Broadcast label. The songs Cutler, Frith and Krause had recorded for the abandoned final session appeared instead as *Hopes and Fears* (1978) by Art Bears, the first of several spin-offs.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-henry-cow.md]

## Rock in Opposition

In March 1978 Henry Cow organised the first **Rock in Opposition** festival in London, inviting Stormy Six (Italy), Samla Mammas Manna (Sweden), Univers Zero (Belgium) and Etron Fou Leloublan (France) — groups that, like them, opposed the hegemony of the Anglo-American rock industry, drew on folk and 20th-century "art music", and often sang in their own languages. After a last concert in Milan on 25 July 1978 the band disbanded; Cutler then launched Recommended Records as a non-commercial distribution network.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-henry-cow.md]

The band is often associated with the [[canterbury-scene|Canterbury scene]] through shared personnel and its links to Soft Machine circles, though its music leaned further toward avant-garde jazz and political text-work than the Canterbury mainstream.
