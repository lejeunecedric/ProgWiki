# Krautrock

**Krautrock** (originally known as *kosmische Musik*, German for "cosmic music") is a broad genre of experimental rock that developed in West Germany in the late 1960s and early 1970s. It blends psychedelic rock, avant-garde composition and electronic music, and is defined less by a single sound than by a shared rejection of Anglo-American rock conventions.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-krautrock.md]

## Definition & origins
- The term "krautrock" was popularised by British music journalists as a pejorative umbrella label for the diverse German scene; many of the artists disliked it, but it fell into standard usage.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-krautrock.md]
- The movement grew partly out of the radical student protests of 1968, as German youth rebelled against their country's Nazi legacy and sought a popular music distinct from both traditional German music and American pop.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-krautrock.md]
- Prominent groups associated with the label included [[canterbury-scene|Can]], [[krautrock|Neu!]], Faust, Tangerine Dream, Kraftwerk, Cluster, Ash Ra Tempel, Popol Vuh, Amon Düül II and Harmonia.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-krautrock.md]

## Musical hallmarks
- **The motorik beat** — a hypnotic, kick-drum-heavy 4/4 pulse that creates a forward-flowing feel, pioneered by Can's drummer Jaki Liebezeit and Neu!'s Klaus Dinger (who called it the "Apache beat").^[raw/articles/wikipedia-motorik.md]
- **Long drones and repetition** — emphasis on long-form repetition, texture and drone rather than conventional song structure, with extended improvisation.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-krautrock.md]
- **Detachment from blues roots** — the music generally moved away from the rhythm & blues roots and song structure of traditional Anglo-American rock.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-krautrock.md]
- **Early synthesizers and musique concrète** — use of synthesizers, tape manipulation and musique concrète techniques.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-krautrock.md]
- AllMusic described it as expanding on art rock and progressive rock territory but diverging from the Anglo-American emphasis on jazz and classical elements in favour of "a droning, pulsating sound that owed more to the avant garde than to rock & roll."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-krautrock.md]

## Representative bands
- **[[canterbury-scene|Can]]** — formed in Cologne in 1968; blended psychedelic rock, funk, samba and musique concrète on *Tago Mago* (1971), *Ege Bamyasi* (1972) and *Future Days* (1973); members had studied with Karlheinz Stockhausen.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-krautrock.md]
- **[[krautrock|Neu!]]** — formed in Düsseldorf in 1971 by Klaus Dinger and Michael Rother after leaving Kraftwerk; pioneered the motorik beat and influenced David Bowie, Brian Eno, Joy Division and Stereolab.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-krautrock.md]
- **Faust** — formed in Hamburg in 1970; worked around dissonance, improvisation and experimental electronic approaches.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-krautrock.md]
- **Kraftwerk** — used the motorik beat on "Autobahn"; later became the defining electronic act.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-motorik.md]

## Legacy
Krautrock influenced post-punk (Joy Division, Siouxsie and the Banshees, Wire, Gang of Four, Cabaret Voltaire), post-rock (Stereolab, Mogwai) and electronic music, and fed into David Bowie's Berlin Trilogy.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-motorik.md]

## Relationship to other sub-styles
Krautrock's *kosmische Musik* wing is closely related to [[space-rock|space rock]] — the term was used as a German analogue to the English "space rock" — and to [[analogue-synthesizer-texture|analogue synthesizer texture]], since the style was often instrumental, "spacy" and built on EMS VCS 3 and Moog Modular synthesizers.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-motorik.md]
