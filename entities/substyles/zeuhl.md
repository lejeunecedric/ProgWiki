# Zeuhl

**Zeuhl** (pronounced [zœl]; lit. "celestial") is a music genre — a hybrid of jazz fusion, symphonic rock and neoclassical music — established in 1969 by the French band Magma. ^[raw/articles/zeuhl.md]

## Origins

Zeuhl was invented by Magma's drummer and leader Christian Vander, who claimed as his inspiration a "vision of humanity's spiritual and ecological future." The term comes from Kobaïan, the fictional language Vander created with vocalist Klaus Blasquiz for Magma, in which *Zeuhl Wortz* means approximately "celestial force." ^[raw/articles/zeuhl.md]

## The Kobaïan language

Most Magma lyrics are sung in Kobaïan, a constructed language based on elements of Slavic and Germanic languages and the scat-yodeling vocal style of Leon Thomas. Vander said the sounds "came naturally with" the music rather than being intellectualised. The first album tells of a people fleeing a doomed Earth to settle on the planet Kobaïa. ^[raw/articles/zeuhl.md]

## Musical hallmarks

- Dominant rhythm: a pumping bass guitar and sometimes sluggish or flexibly playing drum kits
- Slow, repetitive structures that build a hypnotic atmosphere
- Solo passages of high technical finesse
- Vocals ranging from polyphonic choral movements (as in Carl Orff's *Carmina Burana*) to soloistic shrill intonation
- Ceremonial, chanted, martial quality

Dominique Leone of *Pitchfork* called it "what you'd expect an alien rock opera to sound like: massed, chanted choral motifs, martial, repetitive percussion, sudden bursts of explosive improv." Vander has said zeuhl means "the spirit through matter" and is "the sound which you can feel vibrating in your belly." ^[raw/articles/zeuhl.md]

## Reception and influence

Originally applied solely to Magma, the term was extended to similar French bands from the 1970s onward, and to Japanese bands such as Happy Family, Kōenji Hyakkei and Ruins, and French band Zao. Zeuhl's heavy, hypnotic rhythm and ceremonial chanting have been cited as an influence on avant-prog and on the heavier end of [[progressive-metal|progressive metal]], including [[tool|Tool]]. ^[raw/articles/zeuhl.md]
