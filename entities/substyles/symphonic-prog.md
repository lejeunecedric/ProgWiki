# Symphonic Prog

**Symphonic prog** (also called **symphonic progressive rock** or **symphonic rock**) is a subgenre of [[progressive-rock|progressive rock]] that adopts the scope, structure, and tonal palette of Western classical music while retaining a rock band's instrumentation and power. ^[raw/articles/symphonic-prog.md]

## Definition

Symphonic prog is characterised by long, multi-movement suites; recurring leitmotifs; dramatic dynamics; and richly layered keyboard textures — [[mellotron|Mellotron]], [[hammond-organ|Hammond organ]], [[moog-synthesizer|Minimoog]]. Arrangements often imitate orchestral writing through counterpoint, thematic development, and sectional contrast, with guitars and keyboards sharing lead roles much like orchestral sections. ^[raw/articles/symphonic-prog.md] It is the "classic prog" mainstream: [[yes|Yes]], [[genesis|Genesis]], [[emerson-lake-palmer|ELP]] and [[camel|Camel]], contrasted with the more austere [[king-crimson|King Crimson]]. ^[raw/articles/progressive-rock.md]

## Signature elements

- Orchestral textures achieved with keyboards (Mellotron choir/strings, Hammond, Moog) rather than live orchestras
- Long suites with recurring motifs and thematic transformations
- Counterpoint and chamber-style interplay between guitar and keys
- Virtuoso soloing and dramatic dynamic shifts
- Literary, mythic or philosophical lyrics

Only a minority of groups (ELP, Renaissance, Ekseption) directly emulated classical themes; most symphonic bands borrowed orchestral *technique* rather than literal classical compositions. ^[raw/articles/symphonic-prog.md]

## The classic era

The style reached maturity in the early 1970s with ambitious concept albums and side-long compositions by Yes, Genesis, ELP and Renaissance. These bands — the "Big Four" of British symphonic prog — carried the genre to worldwide success in the first half of the decade)Skip. Contrasted with the jazz-rooted [[canterbury-scene|Canterbury scene]] and the avant-garde [[rock-in-opposition|Rock in Opposition]], symphonic prog was the orchestral, melodic, commercially successful mainstream of the genre. ^[raw/articles/progressive-rock.md]

The genre declined in the late 1970s amid the punk backlashREADME (see "Other topics"). Neo-prog in the 1980s and Scandinavian/American acts (The Flower Kings, Spock's Beard, Transatlantic) revived symphonic values in the 1990s and 2000s. ^[raw/articles/symphonic-prog.md]
