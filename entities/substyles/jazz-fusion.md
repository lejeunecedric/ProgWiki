# Jazz Fusion

**Jazz fusion** (also jazz rock, jazz-rock fusion, or simply fusion) is a genre developed in the late 1960s when musicians combined jazz harmony and improvisation with rock, funk and R&B.^[raw/articles/jazz-fusion.md]

## Definition and sound
- Fusion bands typically favour electric guitars, electric pianos, organs, synthesizers and bass guitar over the acoustic piano and double bass of jazz; brass and woodwinds appear as in jazz but are often substituted.^[raw/articles/jazz-fusion.md]
- The term "jazz rock" is used as a synonym both for fusion and for late-1960s/70s rock bands that added jazz elements.^[raw/articles/jazz-fusion.md]
- The label was reportedly coined in a 1968 review of David Axelrod's *Song of Innocence*.^[raw/articles/jazz-fusion.md]
- Milestones include the heavily-edited side-long improvised suites made by Corea, Hancock, Tony Williams, Wayne Shorter, Joe Zawinul and John McLaughlin, and Weather Report, founded December 1970 by Zawinul and Shorter.^[raw/articles/jazz-fusion.md]

## The prog connection
- Progressive rock "with its affinity for long solos, diverse influences, non-standard time signatures, and complex music had very similar musical values as jazz fusion".^[raw/articles/jazz-fusion.md]
- British prog absorbed jazz via Traffic, Colosseum, If, [[soft-machine|Soft Machine]] and Caravan; North American hybrids include Frank Zappa, Return to Forever and the Dixie Dregs.^[raw/articles/progressive-rock.md]
- [[king-crimson|King Crimson]]'s *Larks' Tongues in Aspic* instrumental pieces carry strong jazz fusion and European free-improvisation influences.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-larks-tongues-in-aspic.md]
- [[phil-collins|Phil Collins]] joined the fusion group Brand X; [[bill-bruford|Bill Bruford]] guested with them in 1976.^[raw/articles/phil-collins.md] ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-bill-bruford.md]
- [[zeuhl|Zeuhl]] is itself defined as a hybrid of jazz fusion, symphonic rock and neoclassical music, established in 1969 by Magma.^[raw/articles/zeuhl.md]
- Later progressive metal experiments with fusion appear in Watchtower's *Control and Resistance* (1989), Cynic's *Focus*, Meshuggah, Planet X and The Mars Volta.^[raw/articles/jazz-fusion.md]
