# Rock in Opposition

**Rock in Opposition** (RIO) was a movement representing a collective of progressive rock bands in the late 1970s, united in their opposition to a music industry that refused to recognise their music. It was initiated by the English avant-prog group Henry Cow in March 1978. ^[raw/articles/rock-in-opposition.md]

## Origins

Henry Cow spent most of their last five years touring mainland Europe, where they encountered innovative groups virtually unknown outside their own countries. Their contract with [[virgin-records|Virgin Records]] was cancelled when Virgin realised the band were not profitable. In early 1978, manager Nick Hobbs invited four mainland European groups to tour the UK with Henry Cow under the banner "Rock in Opposition." ^[raw/articles/rock-in-opposition.md]

## The 1978 festival

With a £1000 grant from the British Arts Council, Hobbs organised a RIO festival in London on 12 March 1978 at the New London Theatre, with the slogan "The music the record companies don't want you to hear." The five performing groups were:

- Henry Cow (England)
- Stormy Six (Italy)
- Samla Mammas Manna (Sweden)
- Univers Zero (Belgium)
- Etron Fou Leloublan (France)

^[raw/articles/rock-in-opposition.md]

## The collective

In December 1978 the movement reconstituted as a collective at Sunrise Studio in Kirchberg, Switzerland. Membership was to remain closed and small; new members had to adhere to "musical excellence," work "outside the music business," and hold a "social commitment to Rock." Three new members were elected: Art Zoyd (France), Art Bears (England, ex-Henry Cow members) and Aksak Maboul (Belgium). A second RIO festival took place in Milan in April–May 1979, with further festivals in Sweden and Belgium. RIO ceased as an organisation by the end of 1979. ^[raw/articles/rock-in-opposition.md]

## Recommended Records

Towards the end of 1978, Chris Cutler of Henry Cow and Art Bears established Recommended Records (RēR), an independent label and distribution network for RIO and similar artists. When RIO folded, RēR continued its work as a "virtual" RIO, representing marginalised musicians. ^[raw/articles/rock-in-opposition.md]

## RIO as a genre

After 1979, "RIO" became a minor genre category used to classify a particular subset of avant-garde artists. Avant-prog appeared in the late 1970s as the extension of two prog sub-styles: RIO and the [[canterbury-scene|Canterbury scene]]. ^[raw/articles/rock-in-opposition.md]
