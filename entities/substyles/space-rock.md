# Space Rock

**Space rock** is a music genre characterized by loose and lengthy song structures centred on instrumental textures that typically produce a hypnotic, otherworldly sound. It may feature distorted and reverberation-laden guitars, minimal drumming, languid vocals, synthesizers, and lyrical themes of outer space and science fiction.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]

## Definition & origins
- The genre emerged in late-1960s psychedelia and progressive rock bands such as [[pink-floyd|Pink Floyd]], Hawkwind and Gong who explored a "cosmic" sound.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]
- Similar sounds were pursued in the early-1970s West German *kosmische Musik* ("cosmic music") scene.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]
- Space rock later influenced post-rock and shoegaze.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]

## Musical hallmarks
- **Drones and echo** — distorted, reverberation-laden guitars, minimal drumming and languid vocals create a hypnotic, otherworldly texture.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]
- **"Space" themes** — lyrical and conceptual themes of outer space and science fiction.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]
- **Extended instrumental passages** — loose, lengthy song structures centred on instrumental textures rather than conventional verse-chorus form.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]

## Canonical examples
- **[[pink-floyd|Pink Floyd]]** — early albums contain pioneering examples: "Astronomy Domine", "Pow R. Toc H." and "Interstellar Overdrive" from *The Piper at the Gates of Dawn* (1967), and "Let There Be More Light" and "Set the Controls for the Heart of the Sun" from *A Saucerful of Secrets*, with explicit science-fiction themes.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]
- **Hawkwind** — one of the earliest space rock groups (formed 1969); their 1973 live double album *Space Ritual* was a landmark, documenting a tour with a liquid light show, lasers and science-fiction themes, with Michael Moorcock contributing spoken-word segments.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]
- **Gong** — associated with the [[canterbury-scene|Canterbury scene]], formed in Paris in 1967 by Daevid Allen and Gilli Smyth; their 1973–74 "Radio Gnome Invisible" trilogy established a space-rock mythology.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]

## Relationship to krautrock
Space rock is closely related to [[krautrock|krautrock]]: the German *kosmische Musik* scene was used as a German analogue to the English term "space rock", and both share drones, echo and otherworldly themes.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-space-rock.md]
