# Progressive Metal

**Progressive metal** (often shortened to **prog metal**) is a broad fusion genre melding heavy metal and progressive rock, combining the loud aggression and amplified distorted-guitar sound of the former with the more experimental, cerebral or quasi-classical compositions of the latter.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]

## Definition & origins
- The genre emerged towards the late 1980s but only achieved widespread success in the 1990s.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]
- Its origins date back to the beginning of heavy metal and progressive rock, when some bands began merging the two approaches.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]
- 1960s pioneers [[king-crimson|King Crimson]] maintained musical innovation while incorporating a harder approach, using dissonance and experimental tones while retaining a relationship to hard-rock power chords — the main example being "21st Century Schizoid Man".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]

## Musical hallmarks
- **Virtuosity** — the music typically showcases the extreme technical proficiency of the performers.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]
- **Odd metres and complex rhythm** — unorthodox harmonies, complex rhythms with frequent meter changes and intense syncopation.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]
- **Concept albums** — lyrics draw on fantasy and literature, similar to 1970s progressive rock, and concept albums are a recurring form.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]

## Representative bands
- **[[rush|Rush]]** — the Canadian trio is widely recognized as bridging the gap between hard rock, English progressive rock and pure heavy metal; *2112* (1976) showcased technical expertise and complex composition with a heavier approach.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]
- **Queensrÿche** — a leader of the mid-to-late-1980s progressive metal scene; *Operation: Mindcrime* (1988) is widely considered among the greatest heavy metal concept albums.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]
- [[dream-theater|Dream Theater]] — with *Images and Words* (1992) and *Awake* (1994), helped institutionalize classic progressive metal; one of the genre's "big three" along with Queensrÿche and Fates Warning.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]
- **[[tool|Tool]]** — emerged with a classic heavy metal sound on *Undertow* (1993) before becoming a dominant act in alternative and progressive metal with *Ænima* (1996) and *Lateralus* (2001); toured with King Crimson in 2001.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]

## Legacy
Progressive metal has developed non-linearly, with subgenres such as djent and fusions with death metal and metalcore. It carries forward prog's [[virtuosity|virtuosity]], [[odd-time-signatures|odd metres]] and [[concept-album|concept albums]] into a heavier idiom.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-progressive-metal.md]
