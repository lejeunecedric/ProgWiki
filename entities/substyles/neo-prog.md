# Neo-Prog

**Neo-progressive rock** (commonly abbreviated **neo-prog**) is a subgenre of progressive rock that developed in the UK in the early 1980s. Its most popular band, Marillion, achieved mainstream success in the decade.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]

## Definition & origins
- Neo-prog surfaced in the UK in the early 1980s, drawing on the first wave of progressive rock — early [[genesis|Genesis]], [[camel|Camel]], and to a lesser extent [[van-der-graaf-generator|Van der Graaf Generator]] and [[pink-floyd|Pink Floyd]] — alongside funk, hard rock and punk.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]
- It peaked in the mid-1980s, when the British neo-progressive bands all released material.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]

## Musical hallmarks
- **Symphonic template** — the music is mostly the product of careful composition, relying less on improvised jamming, and built on clean, melodic electric guitar solos with keyboards.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]
- **Emotional, theatrical delivery** — deeply emotional content, dramatic lyrics, imagery and theatricality on stage.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]
- **Refined, radio-friendly tech** — shorter and less complex songs than 1970s prog, with digital synthesizers taking over roles formerly filled by Mellotrons and organs.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]

## Representative bands
- **Marillion** — the genre's most commercially successful band; produced eight UK Top 10 albums between 1983 and 1994, peaking with *Misplaced Childhood* (1985), which topped the UK Albums Chart and produced the hit single "Kayleigh".^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]
- **IQ** — *The Wake* is a famous neo-prog album.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]
- **Pendragon** — *Fly High Fall Far*.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]
- **Pallas** — *The Sentinel*.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]
- **Twelfth Night** — *Art and Illusion*.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]

## Legacy
Neo-prog never reached the heights of the 1970s first wave — only Marillion achieved arena status — but several bands have continued to record and tour, with Marillion and ex-singer Fish maintaining large cult followings.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-neo-prog.md]

## Relationship to classic prog
Neo-prog is a revival of the [[symphonic-prog|symphonic]] template of classic prog, updated for the 1980s. See the [[classic-prog-vs-neo-prog|classic prog vs neo-prog]] comparison for a side-by-side analysis.
