# Rickenbacker 4001 Bass

## Overview

The Rickenbacker 4001 is an electric bass manufactured by Rickenbacker from 1961 to 1986. Designer Roger Rossmeisl created the 4001 as a two-pickup "deluxe" version of Rickenbacker's first production bass, the single-pickup model 4000. The 4001 sported the same unique body shape and "crested wave" headstock of the 4000, with a neck-through-body construction that distinguishes it from most other basses.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-rickenbacker-4001.md]

## The progressive rock sound

In the 1970s, 4001 and 4001S sales were given a boost by [[yes|Yes]] bassist [[chris-squire|Chris Squire]], who popularized using roundwound strings on his bass to create a bright, twangy tone. Squire's tone inspired a number of other progressive rock bassists to use the 4001, including Geddy Lee of [[rush|Rush]], who bought one with his advance following the band signing its first record deal in 1974. The 4001 finished the 1970s as Rickenbacker's bestselling instrument.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-rickenbacker-4001.md]

## Notable players

Many notable rock bassists have played a 4001 or 4001S, including Paul McCartney, John Entwistle, Roger Waters, and Chris Squire. Rickenbacker produced a signature "Chris Squire" model bass (4001CS) for Yes's Squire.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-rickenbacker-4001.md]

## Why it matters in prog

The 4001's bright, cutting tone and melodic potential made it the archetypal bass of [[symphonic-prog|symphonic progressive rock]], where the bass often plays a lead, contrapuntal role rather than a purely rhythmic one. Squire's melodic, elaborately contoured bass lines on the 4001 became a defining element of Yes's sound.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chris-squire.md]
