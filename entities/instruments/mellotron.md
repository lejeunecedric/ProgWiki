# Mellotron

The **Mellotron** is an electro-mechanical keyboard instrument developed in Birmingham, England, in 1963. Pressing a key causes a length of magnetic tape to contact a capstan, which pulls it across a playback head; releasing the key lets a spring retract the tape. Different portions of the tape can be played for different sounds.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-mellotron.md]

## How it works
The Mellotron uses the same concept as a sampler, but generates its sound using analogue recordings on audio tape. Because replaying a tape creates minor fluctuations in pitch (wow and flutter) and amplitude, a note sounds slightly different each time it is played. Pressing a key harder brings the head into contact under greater pressure, so the instrument responds to aftertouch.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-mellotron.md]

## Key models
- **Mk I** (1963) — first commercial model, double manual.
- **Mk II** (1964) — full set of sounds selectable by banks and stations.
- **M400** (1970) — 35-note single manual with a removable tape frame; the most common and portable model, with about 1,800 made.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-mellotron.md]
- **M4000** (2007) — produced by the revived Streetly Electronics, combining the M400 layout with earlier bank selection.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-mellotron.md]

## The signature prog texture
During the 1970s the Mellotron became common in progressive rock, used by groups such as [[king-crimson|King Crimson]], [[yes|Yes]], and [[genesis|Genesis]]. The Moody Blues' Mike Pinder used it extensively on *Days of Future Passed* (1967).^[raw/articles/wikipedia-mellotron.md] In King Crimson, Ian McDonald suggested the group purchase a Mellotron (inspired by the Moody Blues), and it became a key component of the early Crimson sound.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md] The instrument's choir, string, and flute sounds became the archetypal "prog" texture, though its wow-and-flutter instability was part of its charm — Robert Fripp famously quipped that "tuning a Mellotron doesn't."^[raw/articles/wikipedia-mellotron.md]

## Decline and revival
Production ceased in 1986 as polyphonic synthesizers and samplers took over, but the Mellotron regained popularity in the 1990s, used by Oasis, the Smashing Pumpkins, Muse, and Radiohead, leading to the resurrection of Streetly Electronics.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-mellotron.md]
