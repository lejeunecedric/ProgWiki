# Double-Neck Guitar

The **double-neck guitar** is a guitar with two necks sharing a single body, most commonly a 6-string and a 12-string neck (a "6/12"). In progressive rock it functions as an onstage centerpiece and a tool of [[virtuosity]], letting a guitarist switch between textures mid-song without changing instruments. ^[raw/articles/gibson-eds-1275.md]

## Key facts
- The archetypal model is the Gibson EDS-1275, a solid-body double-neck introduced in 1963 (the double-neck concept dates to Gibson's 1958 hollow-body models) and still in production.^[raw/articles/gibson-eds-1275.md]
- Popularized and raised to iconic status by Jimmy Page of Led Zeppelin, John McLaughlin and Don Felder; it has been called "the coolest guitar in rock".^[raw/articles/gibson-eds-1275.md]
- Page used the EDS-1275 live for "Stairway to Heaven", switching between the 6-string and 12-string necks within the song.^[raw/articles/gibson-eds-1275.md]
- Other notable users include Alex Lifeson of [[rush|Rush]] (for "Xanadu") and Eddie Van Halen.^[raw/articles/gibson-eds-1275.md]

## In progressive rock
In the prog context the double-neck sits squarely in the [[virtuosity]] and onstage-centerpiece space: it is a visual and musical statement of technical ambition. Genesis guitarist [[steve-hackett|Steve Hackett]] was associated with double-neck and 12-string guitar work, and the instrument's 6/12 configuration suits the layered, chordal textures of [[symphonic-prog]].^[raw/articles/steve-hackett.md]^[raw/articles/gibson-eds-1275.md]
