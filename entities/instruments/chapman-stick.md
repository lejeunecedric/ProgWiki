# Chapman Stick

The **Chapman Stick** is an electric string instrument devised by Emmett Chapman in the early 1970s. A member of the guitar family, it usually has ten or twelve individually tuned strings and is used to play bass lines, melody lines, chords, or textures; designed as a fully polyphonic chordal instrument, it can cover several of these musical parts simultaneously.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chapman-stick.md]

## How it works
Unlike a guitar, which is strummed or plucked, the Chapman Stick is played by tapping: the player uses both hands to sound notes by striking the strings against the fingerboard, in a technique similar to playing a piano. This allows many notes to be sounded at once, making the instrument more comparable to a keyboard in terms of polyphony, and lends itself to playing bass, chords, and melody lines simultaneously.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chapman-stick.md] The instrument is set up with very little relief in the fretboard — very flat compared to a guitar — which suits the tapping style.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chapman-stick.md]

## History
In 1969, jazz guitarist Emmett Chapman developed the two-handed tapping technique (both hands playing parallel to the frets) and applied it to his playing, then developed a new instrument to use the method's full potential. The first production model of the Stick was launched in 1974.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chapman-stick.md]

## The prog "super-instrument"
The Chapman Stick became a signature instrument of progressive rock through its use in [[king-crimson|King Crimson]]. Session player Tony Levin was an early user from the mid-1970s, bringing it to sessions and tours with Peter Gabriel and featuring it in his work as a member of King Crimson from 1981 onwards.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chapman-stick.md] The 1981 King Crimson album *Discipline* (played by Levin) is cited as an influential recording for the Stick's prominent role.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chapman-stick.md] When bassist Trey Gunn joined King Crimson in 1994, the six-member band operated as a "double trio" with two drummers, two electric guitarists, and two Stick players — Levin and Gunn — enabling dense, contrapuntal textures.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chapman-stick.md]^[raw/articles/wikipedia-king-crimson.md]

## Hallmarks
- **Bass + lead in one instrument** — one hand plays melody on the treble strings while the other plays rhythm on the bass strings, giving a single player a two-part texture.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chapman-stick.md]
- **Counterpoint** — the fully polyphonic design suits the interlocking, contrapuntal arrangements of King Crimson's 1980s and 1990s line-ups.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chapman-stick.md]
- **MIDI capability** — with a special synthesizer pickup, the Stick can trigger synthesizers and send MIDI messages to electronic instruments.^[raw/articles/wikipedia-chapman-stick.md]
