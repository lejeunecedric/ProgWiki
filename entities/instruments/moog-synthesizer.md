# Moog Synthesizer

The **Moog synthesizer** is a modular synthesizer invented by the American engineer **Robert Moog** in 1964 — the first commercial synthesizer and the instrument that established the analog synthesizer concept. Moog's principal innovation was **voltage control** (using voltage to control pitch), along with modularity and envelope generators. The instrument consists of separate modules — voltage-controlled oscillators, amplifiers, filters, envelope generators, noise generators, triggers and mixers — connected by patch cords and shaped through subtractive synthesis. ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-moog-synthesizer.md]

## The Minimoog
In 1970 Moog Music released the **Minimoog**, a portable, self-contained, monophonic version designed as a more affordable alternative to the modular systems. It was the first synthesizer sold in retail stores and the first to feature a pitch wheel, letting players bend notes expressively like a guitarist or saxophonist. Its three oscillators were never fully synchronised, unintentionally producing the instrument's "warm, rich" sound. ^[raw/articles/wikipedia-minimoog.md]

## Role in progressive rock
At its height of popularity the Moog/Minimoog was a **staple of 1970s progressive rock**, used by acts including [[yes]], Tangerine Dream and [[emerson-lake-palmer]]. It became prog's sonic signature for both **texture and lead**: Keith Emerson used it for dramatic solo leads and bass, Rick Wakeman for sweeping textures and choir-like pads, and Pink Floyd for atmospheric glides and shimmer. It sits alongside the [[mellotron]] and [[hammond-organ]] as one of the three defining keyboard instruments of classic prog, and its pad/atmosphere role is the core of [[analogue-synthesizer-texture]].
