Rock in Opposition (RIO) was a movement representing a collective of progressive rock bands in the late 1970s united in their opposition to the music industry. It was initiated by Henry Cow in March 1978, which invited four mainland European groups (Stormy Six, Samla Mammas Manna, Univers Zero, Etron Fou Leloublan) to a festival in London, "Rock in Opposition", with the slogan "The music the record companies don't want you to hear", supported by a 1000 pound grant from the British Arts Council. In December 1978 the movement reconstituted as a collective at Sunrise Studio in Kirchberg, Switzerland; membership was to remain closed and small, members to adhere to "musical excellence", work "outside the music business", and hold "social commitment to Rock". Three new members were elected: Art Zoyd (France), Art Bears (England, ex-Henry Cow members), and Aksak Maboul (Belgium). A second RIO festival took place at the Teatro dell'Elfo in Milan in April-May 1979, and further festivals in Sweden and Belgium. RIO ceased as an organisation by the end of 1979. Chris Cutler's Recommended Records (RER) label continued RIO's work as a "virtual" RIO for marginalised musicians. RIO became a minor genre category after 1979, a means of classifying avant-garde artists; combined with the Canterbury style it created avant-prog.
