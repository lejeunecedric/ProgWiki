**Hatfield and the North** were an [experimental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_rock "Experimental rock") [Canterbury scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_scene "Canterbury scene") rock band that lasted from October 1972 to June 1975, with some reunions thereafter.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_and_the_North#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1)

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hatfield_and_the_North&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Career")\]

In mid 1972, the band grew out of a line-up of ex-members of blues/jazz/rock band [Delivery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delivery_(British_band) "Delivery (British band)"), [Pip Pyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pip_Pyle "Pip Pyle") (drums, who had since played with [Gong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong_(band) "Gong (band)")), [Phil Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Miller "Phil Miller") (guitar, who had joined [Matching Mole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matching_Mole "Matching Mole")), and Phil's brother Steve Miller ( [Wurlitzer electronic piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wurlitzer_electronic_piano "Wurlitzer electronic piano"), who had joined [Caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band) "Caravan (band)")).[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_and_the_North#cite_note-Larkin-2) Replacing [Roy Babbington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Babbington "Roy Babbington") on bass was [Richard Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Sinclair "Richard Sinclair") (who played with Steve Miller in Caravan).[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_and_the_North#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1) This line-up moved away from the blues idiom of the early Delivery towards pieces based on riffs in odd time signatures and protracted melodies associated with the Canterbury style.

The band played a few live shows between July and September that year, and gained their first record contract with [Virgin Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Records "Virgin Records") with the 'Sinclair cousins'...as Steve Miller was replaced by [Dave Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Sinclair "Dave Sinclair") ( [Hammond organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ"), also from Matching Mole and Caravan), the band soon changed their name to Hatfield and the North.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_and_the_North#cite_note-Larkin-2)

Delivery reunited for a BBC session in November 1972 with Steve Miller, Phil Miller, [Lol Coxhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lol_Coxhill "Lol Coxhill"), [Roy Babbington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Babbington "Roy Babbington") (bass), Pip Pyle, and Richard Sinclair on vocals. (Steve Miller went on to release a couple of duo albums with Coxhill in 1973/74.)

Dave Sinclair left in January 1973, shortly after the band's appearance (with [Robert Wyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt "Robert Wyatt") on guest vocals) on the French TV programme _Rockenstock_, and was replaced by [Dave Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Stewart_(musician,_born_1950) "Dave Stewart (musician, born 1950)") (from [Egg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_(band) "Egg (band)")) before the band's first recordings were made.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_and_the_North#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1)

The band recorded two albums: _[Hatfield and the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_and_the_North_(album) "Hatfield and the North (album)")_ (1974) and _[The Rotters' Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rotters'_Club_(album) "The Rotters' Club (album)")_ (1975).[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_and_the_North#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1) [Backing vocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backing_vocals "Backing vocals") on the two albums were sung by The Northettes: Amanda Parsons, [Barbara Gaskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Gaskin "Barbara Gaskin") (who was born in [Hatfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield,_Hertfordshire "Hatfield, Hertfordshire"), the town in [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire "Hertfordshire") referred to in the group's name), and Ann Rosenthal. On the autumn 1974 "Crisis Tour", which Hatfield co-headlined with [Kevin Coyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Coyne "Kevin Coyne"), the opening act was a [duo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duet_(music) "Duet (music)") of Steve Miller and [Lol Coxhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lol_Coxhill "Lol Coxhill") (also previously of Delivery) and Coxhill usually guested with Hatfield on the jamming sections of "Mumps".\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

After disbanding, Dave Stewart formed [National Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health "National Health") with [Alan Gowen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Gowen "Alan Gowen") from [Gilgamesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgamesh_(band) "Gilgamesh (band)");[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_and_the_North#cite_note-Larkin-2) Phil Miller was a member throughout that band's existence, and Pyle joined in 1977. (Richard Sinclair also sat in on a couple of gigs and a BBC radio session that year.) Hatfield and the North and Gilgamesh had played a couple of shows together in late 1973, including a joint "double quartet" set, in some ways the prototype for National Health. Miller, Stewart, Pyle, and Sinclair also worked together in various combinations on other projects.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6a/A1%28M%29_sign_at_junction_1%2C_South_Mimms.jpg/250px-A1%28M%29_sign_at_junction_1%2C_South_Mimms.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:A1(M)_sign_at_junction_1,_South_Mimms.jpg) 2002 roadsign marking "The NORTH, Hatfield"

The name of the band was inspired by the road signage on the main A1 road heading north from London, where there are a succession of signs (such as that formerly at the junction outside the Odeon cinema in [Barnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipping_Barnet "Chipping Barnet")) referred to the first major town, and the overall direction, as 'A1 Hatfield & the North'.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_and_the_North#cite_note-Larkin-2) This style of sign from the 1970s has now been replaced by a slightly different variant, reading "The NORTH, Hatfield".

## Reunions and archival releases

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hatfield_and_the_North&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Reunions and archival releases")\]

In March 1990, the group reformed to record a television programme at [Central Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Meadow_Campus "King's Meadow Campus"), [Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham "Nottingham") on 30 March, with Phil Miller, Richard Sinclair, and Pip Pyle joined by [Sophia Domancich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophia_Domancich "Sophia Domancich") (keyboards, Pyle's then-girlfriend and bandmate in Equip'Out).[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_and_the_North#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1) A live album from that show, titled _[Live 1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_1990_(Hatfield_and_the_North_album) "Live 1990 (Hatfield and the North album)")_, was released in November 1993.

In January 2005, the band reformed again with Alex Maguire (from Pip Pyle's Bash!) on keyboards and toured between 2005 and 2006 (notable appearances included a short Japanese tour in late 2005, and the [Baja Prog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja_Prog "Baja Prog") and [NEARfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEARfest "NEARfest") festivals in North America). On a small number of European dates in June 2005, Mark Fletcher (from Miller's [In Cahoots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Cahoots "In Cahoots") band) reinforced the band while Pyle was recuperating from a back operation and only played on part of each gig. Pyle died in August 2006 after travelling back from a Hatfield show in [Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groningen "Groningen"). Following Pyle's death, Hatfield played two previously booked gigs with Mark Fletcher on drums, including the [Canterbury Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_Festival "Canterbury Festival") in October 2006.

In 2005/2006, the band released two archival collections, _[Hatwise Choice: Archive Recordings 1973–1975, Volume 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatwise_Choice:_Archive_Recordings_1973%E2%80%931975,_Volume_1 "Hatwise Choice: Archive Recordings 1973–1975, Volume 1")_ and _[Hattitude: Archive Recordings 1973–1975, Volume 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattitude:_Archive_Recordings_1973%E2%80%931975,_Volume_2?action=edit&redlink=1 "Hattitude: Archive Recordings 1973–1975, Volume 2 (page does not exist)")_, featuring the classic Miller/Pyle/Sinclair/Stewart line-up, distributed by the UK label [Burning Shed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burning_Shed "Burning Shed"). Both releases contained a mixture of BBC radio sessions and live recordings, along with the odd demo, which are still available on CD and support the musicians and family of Pip Pyle.

In 2007, [Cuneiform Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform_Records "Cuneiform Records") re-released two albums by Steve Miller and [Lol Coxhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lol_Coxhill "Lol Coxhill") with bonus material including 20 minutes of material by the proto-Hatfield and the North line-up of Delivery playing "God Song", "Bossa Nochance/Big Jobs", and "Betty" (a variation on some of the Sinclair bass riffs that also produced Hatfield's "Rifferama").

[Jonathan Coe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Coe "Jonathan Coe")'s 2001 novel _[The Rotters' Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rotters'_Club_(novel) "The Rotters' Club (novel)")_ takes its title from the band's second album. The novel also mentions them several times.

[Saint Etienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Etienne_(band) "Saint Etienne (band)") also reference the band in the track "Popmaster" on their 2017 album _[Home Counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Counties_(album) "Home Counties (album)")_.

In 2022, Richard Sinclair assembled a tribute band of Hatfield and the North, with a particular dedication to Phil Miller and Pip Pyle. The group that made its debut in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo "Palermo") on 10 December 2022 was called the HAT Band, and included Richard Sinclair (vocals and bass), Alex Maguire (electric piano), Mezz Gacano (guitar and backing vocals), and Giulio Scavuzzo (drums).
