Rush is a Canadian rock band, formed in Toronto in 1968. The band's original line-up included guitarist Alex Lifeson, drummer John Rutsey, and bassist and vocalist Jeff Jones, whom Geddy Lee replaced shortly after its formation. Rush went through a few line-up changes over the next six years, before settling on its classic power trio line-up with the addition of drummer Neil Peart in July 1974, replacing Rutsey after the release of their self-titled debut album. The line-up of Lifeson, Lee, and Peart remained stable for the remainder of the band's initial run until 2015, after which Peart retired from music.

Moving Pictures is the eighth studio album by Canadian rock band Rush, released on February 12, 1981.

In January 1976, the band entered Toronto Sound Studios to record with their longtime associate Terry Brown assuming his role as producer, operating a Studer 24-track machine. For the acoustic sections, he plays a 12-string Gibson B-45 and a six-string Gibson Dove. "Tom Sawyer" features a backbeat in a 4/4 time signature, along with instrumental and closing sections in 7/8. It was the first Rush recording for which Lee used his 1972 Fender Jazz Bass.

2112 (pronounced "twenty-one twelve") is the fourth studio album by Canadian rock band Rush, released in March 1976. Following the disappointing sales of their previous albums Caress of Steel and Fly by Night, 2112 became the band's first major commercial success. The band were pressured to deliver more commercial material but decided to continue developing the progressive rock direction they had explored on Caress of Steel and made the 20-minute futuristic science-fiction title track occupy side one of 2112.

Side one is occupied by the 20-minute futuristic science-fiction song "2112". The seven-part track is based on a story by Neil Peart. The song "2112" tells a story set in the city of Megadon in the year 2112, where individualism and creativity are outlawed.
