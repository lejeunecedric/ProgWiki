Virgin Records was a British record label founded in 1972 by Richard Branson, Simon Draper, Nik Powell, and musician Tom Newman, growing to become a major label alongside A&M and Island. Its original logo (the "Gemini"/"Twins" logo) was designed by Roger Dean. The first release on the label was Mike Oldfield's progressive rock album Tubular Bells (1973), for which the fledgling label garnered unprecedented acclaim; soon followed by notable krautrock releases including Tangerine Dream's Phaedra (a Top 20 hit), The Faust Tapes and Faust IV by Faust, and Gong's Flying Teapot (Radio Gnome Invisible, Pt.1). The label initially was one of the key labels of English and European progressive rock, but the 1977 signing of the Sex Pistols reinvented it as a new wave outpost. The current Virgin "scrawl" logo was first used on Mike Oldfield's Incantations album (1978). Virgin Records was sold by Branson to Thorn EMI in June 1992 for a reported US$1 billion. After EMI was taken over by Universal in 2012, Virgin became part of Universal and later an imprint of the rebranded EMI/Virgin EMI Records.
