The Chapman Stick is an electric musical instrument devised by Emmett Chapman in the early 1970s. A member of the guitar family, the Chapman Stick usually has ten or twelve individually tuned strings and is used to play bass lines, melody lines, chords, or textures. Designed as a fully polyphonic chordal instrument, it can also cover several of these musical parts simultaneously.

In contrast to the guitar or bass, the Stick is set up with very little relief in the fretboard. It is very flat compared to a guitar. The Chapman Stick player uses both hands to sound notes by striking the strings against the fingerboard.

Former Weather Report bassist Alphonso Johnson was among the first musicians to introduce the Chapman Stick to the public. Session player Tony Levin was also an early user and was playing the instrument from the mid-1970s: he would bring it to sessions and tours with Peter Gabriel and featured it in his work as a member of King Crimson from 1981 onwards. Levin formed the band Stick Men, consisting of one drummer and two Stick players. When bassist Trey Gunn joined King Crimson in 1994, the six-member band ... with two drummers, two electric guitarists, and two Stick players: Levin and Gunn.

In 1969, jazz guitarist Emmett Chapman developed the two-handed tapping technique (in which both hands play parallel to the frets) and applied it to his playing. The first production model of the Stick was launched in 1974.

Recordings that have been influential on Stick players, because of the prominent role the Stick plays, include the 1981 King Crimson album Discipline (played by Tony Levin).
