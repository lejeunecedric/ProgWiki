Moving Pictures is the eighth studio album by Canadian rock band Rush, released on February 12, 1981, by Anthem Records. After touring to support their previous album Permanent Waves (1980), the band wrote and recorded new material with longtime co-producer Terry Brown, continuing toward a more radio-friendly sound with tighter and shorter song structures.

Moving Pictures received a positive reception and became an instant commercial success, reaching number one in Canada and number three in both the United States and the United Kingdom. It is the fastest-selling album in the band's catalogue, certified Platinum by the RIAA on April 27, 1981 for one million copies sold just two months after release, and has since been certified five times Platinum in the US.

"Tom Sawyer" features a backbeat in a 4/4 time signature, along with instrumental and closing sections in 7/8. It was the first Rush recording for which Lee used his 1972 Fender Jazz Bass, which provided a punchier lower end than his usual Rickenbacker 4001. The song grew from what Lee would play on his synthesizer during sound checks on tour.

"YYZ" is an instrumental titled after the IATA airport code for Toronto Pearson International Airport; its rhythm is that of the letters "YYZ" in Morse code. It stemmed from the band's enjoyment of recording "La Villa Strangiato", a nine-minute instrumental on Hemispheres (1978).

"Limelight" is autobiographical, based on Peart's dissatisfaction with fame. "The Camera Eye" is a two-part track with sections unofficially titled "New York City" and "London"; it was the final song the band included on a studio album with a length of over ten minutes. "Vital Signs" features a sequencer part produced by an Oberheim OB-X synthesizer and shows a distinct reggae flavour.

The album was recorded at Le Studio in Morin-Heights, Quebec in October and November 1980, using 48-track recording for the first time, and mixed down onto a Sony digital mastering machine — an early example of digital recording. In 2020, Rolling Stone ranked the album number 379 on its list of the "500 Greatest Albums of All Time".
