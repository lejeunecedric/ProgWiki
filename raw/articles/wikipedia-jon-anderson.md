Jon Anderson (born John Roy Anderson, 25 October 1944) is an English singer, songwriter and musician who co-founded the progressive rock band Yes with bassist Chris Squire in 1968 and rose to prominence as their lead vocalist. The band pioneered progressive rock in the 1970s, particularly with their critically acclaimed albums The Yes Album, Fragile (both 1971) and Close to the Edge (1972). Known for his high tenor vocal style, Anderson was a member of Yes across three tenures: from 1968 to 1980, 1983 to 1988, and 1990 to 2008.

In May 1968, Anderson met Chris Squire, bassist of the London-based rock band Mabel Greer's Toyshop; the two shared common musical interests including Simon & Garfunkel and the idea of vocal harmonies. In June 1968 Anderson and Squire hired Bill Bruford to replace drummer Robert Haggerholi. The group renamed themselves Yes, originally Banks's idea, and Anderson performed his first gig with Yes on 3 August 1968.

Although the band had no formal leader, Anderson served as its main motivating force in their early days, doing most of the hustling for gigs and originating most of their songs. He played a key role in initiating their more ambitious artistic ideas, serving as the main instigator of some of the band's more popular songs, including 'Close to the Edge', 'The Gates of Delirium', and 'Awaken', and the concept behind the double concept album Tales from Topographic Oceans (1973).

In 1974, Anderson co-wrote 'Pearly Gates' with Iron Butterfly drummer Ron Bushy. In 2009 he became an American citizen, and in 2017 he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Yes.
