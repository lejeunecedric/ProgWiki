Charisma Records (also known as The Famous Charisma Label) was a British record label founded in 1969 by former journalist Tony Stratton Smith. He had previously acted as manager for rock bands such as the Nice, the Bonzo Dog Band and Van der Graaf Generator.

The label's most successful acts were Genesis, Peter Gabriel, Julian Lennon, and Monty Python. The first release was the eponymous debut LP by Rare Bird, in December 1969, who gave Charisma its first hit single, "Sympathy", in early 1970. Besides the Nice, the Bonzo Dog Band and Van der Graaf Generator, the label also released material by Lindisfarne, Hawkwind, the Alan Parsons Project, String Driven Thing, Audience, and Brand X among others. The 1970s solo albums of Peter Hammill, Tony Banks and Steve Hackett were also on Charisma.

Charisma's first UK label was a distinctive magenta scroll design ("pink scroll"), used from 1969 until mid-1972. Its second logo, used beginning in 1972, of John Tenniel's drawing of the Mad Hatter, made the label instantly recognizable.

In 1983, Charisma Records was acquired by Virgin Records and continued to operate until 1986, when Virgin absorbed the label.
