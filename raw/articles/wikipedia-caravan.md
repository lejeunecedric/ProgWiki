**Caravan** are an English rock band from the [Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury "Canterbury") area, founded by former [Wilde Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wilde_Flowers "The Wilde Flowers") members [David Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Sinclair "Dave Sinclair"), [Richard Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Sinclair "Richard Sinclair"), [Pye Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pye_Hastings "Pye Hastings"), and [Richard Coughlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Coughlan "Richard Coughlan") in 1968.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-AMGBio-1) The band have never achieved the great commercial success that was widely predicted for them at the beginning of their career, but are nevertheless considered a key part of the [Canterbury scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_scene "Canterbury scene") of [progressive rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_rock "Progressive rock") acts, blending [psychedelic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_rock "Psychedelic rock"), [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"), and [classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music "Classical music") influences to create a distinctive sound.

The band were originally based in [Whitstable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitstable "Whitstable"), [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent "Kent"), near Canterbury, but moved to London when briefly signed to [Verve Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verve_Records "Verve Records"). After being dropped by Verve, the band signed to [Decca Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decca_Records "Decca Records"), where they released their most critically acclaimed album, _[In the Land of Grey and Pink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Land_of_Grey_and_Pink "In the Land of Grey and Pink")_, in 1971. Dave Sinclair left after the album's release and the group split up the following year. Hastings and Coughlan added new members, notably viola player [Geoffrey Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Richardson_(musician) "Geoffrey Richardson (musician)"), continuing on before splitting in 1978.

The band re-formed several times in the following decades, and Caravan still remain active as a live band in the 21st century, despite Coughlan's death in December 2013.

## History

### Early career

The group's original members, [David Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Sinclair_(keyboardist) "Dave Sinclair (keyboardist)"), [Richard Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Sinclair "Richard Sinclair"), [Pye Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pye_Hastings "Pye Hastings") and [Richard Coughlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Coughlan "Richard Coughlan"), had all been in the Canterbury-based [Wilde Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilde_Flowers "Wilde Flowers"), albeit not at the same time. Richard Sinclair had been an early member, but left in September 1965 to study at college.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBennett200561-2) Hastings had replaced [Robert Wyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt "Robert Wyatt") as the group's singer and Coughlan as drummer in the band when Wyatt, who performed both roles, formed [Soft Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Machine "Soft Machine").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBennett200567-3) David Sinclair joined the group in late 1966, but after future Soft Machine member [Hugh Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Hopper "Hugh Hopper") left the group in June the following year, they began to run out of momentum and broke up in October 1967.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBennett200568-4)

Coughlan, Hastings and the two Sinclairs subsequently formed Caravan in 1968. "We all had the same goal" recalled Richard Sinclair, "to make our music, write it ourselves, and make a living from it."[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERomano2010192-5) The band rented a house in [Whitstable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitstable "Whitstable"), [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent "Kent") for six months, where they began to write and rehearse new material.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-greypink_sleeve-6) They also borrowed Soft Machine's PA for rehearsals while that band was on tour with [Jimi Hendrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimi_Hendrix "Jimi Hendrix") in the US, as Caravan did not have enough funds for their own equipment.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBennett2005127-7) They were forced to leave in June and ended up living in tents and rehearsing in a local church hall.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-greypink_sleeve-6) By October, they had attracted the interest of music publisher Ian Ralfini,[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-ificould-8):4 who signed them to the American record label [Verve Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verve_Records "Verve Records"), the first British act they signed.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-greypink_sleeve-6) Verve subsequently released the band's debut LP, _[Caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(Caravan_album) "Caravan (Caravan album)")_ (1969), the following year, but a few months later moved out of the UK record business and dropped the band.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-ificould-8):3

After a series of gigs in London, including the [Speakeasy Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speakeasy_Club "Speakeasy Club"), the band were introduced to Terry King, who became the group's first manager. [David Hitchcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Hitchcock "Dave Hitchcock"), who had been working in the art department of [Decca Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decca_Records "Decca Records"), asked the company's president, [Hugh Mendl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Mendl "Hugh Mendl"), to sign the band.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-ificould-8):4 They began recording their second album, _[If I Could Do It All Over Again, I'd Do It All Over You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If_I_Could_Do_It_All_Over_Again,_I'd_Do_It_All_Over_You "If I Could Do It All Over Again, I'd Do It All Over You")_ (1970), in September 1969, while continuing to gig on the university circuit, and appearing at festivals alongside [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd"), [Yes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_(band) "Yes (band)"), [The Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nice "The Nice") and [Soft Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Machine "Soft Machine").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-ificould-8):5 Recording _If I could..._ continued in February 1970, with the 14-minute track "For Richard", showing the band's contrast in styles and [jazz-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz-rock "Jazz-rock") influence.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-ificould-8):7 The album was released in August, alongside an appearance at the Plumpton Festival with [Van der Graaf Generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_der_Graaf_Generator "Van der Graaf Generator"), Yes and [Colosseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colosseum_(band) "Colosseum (band)").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-greypink-9):4 The accompanying [single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_(music) "Single (music)") "Hello Hello" helped them land an appearance on the TV show _[Top of the Pops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_of_the_Pops "Top of the Pops")_, performing the album's title track.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-ificould-8):9

Caravan had started to build a live following by mid-1970, including an appearance at the [Kralingen Pop Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kralingen_Music_Festival "Kralingen Music Festival") in the Netherlands to an audience of 250,000 and the 10th [Plumpton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plumpton,_East_Sussex "Plumpton, East Sussex") Festival. In autumn 1970, Caravan began working on one of their most critically acclaimed albums, _[In the Land of Grey and Pink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Land_of_Grey_and_Pink "In the Land of Grey and Pink")_ (1971). The balance of songwriting changed from the previous two albums, with Richard Sinclair taking a more prominent role. His song "Golf Girl" was originally written about his then girlfriend (and future wife), but the lyrics were rewritten in the final version.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-greypink-9):8 The group decided to follow up "For Richard" with a suite of short sections of songs written by David Sinclair, that the rest of the band worked on and linked together to form a side-long track, "Nine Feet Underground". Although the track was recorded in five separate stages and spliced together, the band performed the suite live as it was finally presented on the album, and it remained a popular track in their live set.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-greypink-9):9 The album was released in April 1971, and though it did not chart, it has remained in print ever since, and has been remastered for CD several times,[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-greypink-9):10 notably a digital remaster in 2011 by [Porcupine Tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcupine_Tree "Porcupine Tree")'s [Steven Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Wilson "Steven Wilson").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-10) "Nine Feet Underground" in particular was a regular fixture on late-night FM radio during the early 1970s.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartin1998190-11)

Despite the critical success of _In the Land of Grey and Pink_, the group were disappointed by its lack of commercial success, believing that Decca were not promoting the band properly or investing enough money. In August 1971, David Sinclair accepted a job with former Soft Machine drummer [Robert Wyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt "Robert Wyatt")'s new band, [Matching Mole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matching_Mole "Matching Mole"). Reflecting on the decision to leave, Sinclair later said, "I felt the whole thing was going a bit stagnant... I wanted to play with other people, but had to accept that with Caravan it was either all or nothing."[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-greypink-9):11 Hastings remembers that "Dave's departure was a serious blow."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-waterloo-12):5

### Line-up changes

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Caravan%2C_1974.jpg/500px-Caravan%2C_1974.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Caravan,_1974.jpg) Caravan in 1974. From left to right: Pye Hastings, Geoffrey Richardson, Mike Wedgwood, Richard Coughlan, Dave Sinclair.

The remaining members continued on together and Richard Sinclair invited keyboardist Steve Miller to join the band.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-waterloo-12):5 However, it was immediately apparent that the style of the band would be different. Sinclair and Miller wanted to perform more jazz-rock, while Hastings was frustrated that the previous style was being neglected, and that it was "virtually impossible" to get Miller to perform in David Sinclair's style.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-waterloo-12):8 The band started recording a new album, _[Waterloo Lily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterloo_Lily "Waterloo Lily")_ (1972) in late 1971, which was the first use of orchestral instrumentation, arranged by Hastings and his brother, Jimmy, who had guested on previous albums.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-waterloo-12):9 The album was released in May 1972, but by this time, musical differences had come to a head, and after a gig with [Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genesis_(band) "Genesis (band)") in July, the band split. Richard Sinclair later said, "It didn't quite work with Steve in the band because the music started to go a bit too loose for the way that Pye and Richard Coughlan played."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-waterloo-12):10

Hastings and Coughlan decided to continue as Caravan, and the duo recruited [viola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viola "Viola") player [Geoffrey Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Richardson_(musician) "Geoffrey Richardson (musician)"), bassist Stu Evans and keyboardist Derek Austin and toured extensively. This line-up did not release any recordings before Evans was replaced by [John G. Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_G._Perry "John G. Perry") and Dave Sinclair rejoined the group in 1973.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-AMGBio-1)

The resulting album _[For Girls Who Grow Plump in the Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Girls_Who_Grow_Plump_in_the_Night "For Girls Who Grow Plump in the Night")_ (1973) was a major critical achievement[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-AMGBio-1) and showed that the group could survive the loss of co-vocalist Richard Sinclair. Jimmy Hastings reprised his role as orchestrator along with Martyn Ford and John Bell,[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHinton2003171-13) while the Soft Machine's [Mike Ratledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Ratledge "Mike Ratledge") contributed an instrumental piece, "Backwards" as part of a medley.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERomano2010193-14) The group followed this with a live album, _[Caravan and the New Symphonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_and_the_New_Symphonia "Caravan and the New Symphonia")_ (1974), including orchestral arrangements by Ford[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartin1998222-15) and conducting by Simon Jeffes.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHinton2003171-13)

Perry left after the _New Symphonia_ and was replaced by [Mike Wedgwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Wedgwood "Mike Wedgwood") for the album _[Cunning Stunts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cunning_Stunts_(Caravan_album) "Cunning Stunts (Caravan album)")_ (1975), which reached the top 50 in the UK[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-16) and was a minor hit in the US, reaching number 124.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-AMGBio-1) It was the last album released on Decca, which preceded a number of other problems in the band. David Sinclair left after the album was recorded and was replaced by [Jan Schelhaas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Schelhaas "Jan Schelhaas"), with the band's sound becoming more mainstream.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHinton2003171-13) The group signed to [Miles Copeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Copeland_III "Miles Copeland III")'s BTM Records[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-AMGBio-1) and recorded _[Blind Dog at St. Dunstans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_Dog_at_St._Dunstans "Blind Dog at St. Dunstans")_ (1976), which was another minor album hit,[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-17) but by this point the group were out of step with the prevailing musical trends, and after a final album, _[Better by Far](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_by_Far "Better by Far")_ (1977) on [Arista Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arista_Records "Arista Records"),[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-AMGBio-1) the group split.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHinton2003171-13)

### 1980-2000

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/PyeHastingsBC.jpg/330px-PyeHastingsBC.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:PyeHastingsBC.jpg) Pye Hastings performing with Caravan at the High Voltage Festival in 2011

In 1980 Caravan resumed recording and activity and recorded _[The Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Album_(Caravan_album) "The Album (Caravan album)")_ with a lineup that included Dek Messecar on bass and vocals; [David Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Sinclair "Dave Sinclair"), keyboards; [Richard Coughlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Coughlan "Richard Coughlan"), drums; [Geoff Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Richardson_(musician) "Geoffrey Richardson (musician)"), viola, guitars, vocals, and flute; and [Pye Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pye_Hastings "Pye Hastings"), guitar and vocals. The single taken from "Heartbreaker" returned the group to the charts in the UK and they had some TV appearances in France. Although this formation did not last long, since the following year the group was restructured again with the original formation and they recorded a version of "Back to Front" with the addition of [Mel Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Collins "Mel Collins") on sax.
The band were largely dormant in the 1980s until a 1990 reunion, planned as a one-off for television, reinvigorated their career.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHinton2003171-13) The group continued to tour in the early 1990s, before [Richard Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Sinclair "Richard Sinclair") left. He was replaced by [Jim Leverton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Leverton "Jim Leverton"), while Geoffrey Richardson rejoined the band. This version of the group released _[The Battle of Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battle_of_Hastings_(album) "The Battle of Hastings (album)")_ in 1995.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHinton2003171-13)

### 21st century

The group continued to play into the 21st century. They have also achieved steady sales and a fan following with the support of [Stuart Maconie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Maconie "Stuart Maconie")'s "Freak Zone" show on [BBC 6 Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_6_Music "BBC 6 Music").[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-jackman-18) After performing at [NEARFest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEARFest "NEARFest") in 2002,[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERomano2010193-14) they released _[The Unauthorized Breakfast Item](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Unauthorized_Breakfast_Item "The Unauthorized Breakfast Item")_ album in 2003, where David Sinclair was replaced by a returning Jan Schelhaas. An archive collection of BBC sessions from 1968 to 1975, _[The Show of Our Lives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Show_of_Our_Lives_%E2%80%93_Caravan_at_the_BBC_1968%E2%80%931975 "The Show of Our Lives – Caravan at the BBC 1968–1975")_, was released in 2007.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-jackman-18)

In 2010, Pye Hastings announced that the band had resumed activity in anticipation of a one-off concert recording at [Metropolis Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_Group "Metropolis Group") for ITV, which took place in December 2010. New material was written for a debut performance and the band was joined by Mark Walker on drums and percussion, as Richard Coughlan, whilst still a member of the band, was too ill to tour with the group.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-obit-19) The DVD of this gig was released in May 2011 and the recording was shown on ITV as part of the Legends series.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-20)

In January 2013, the band completed a successful UK tour to celebrate the 40th anniversary of the album _[For Girls Who Grow Plump in the Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Girls_Who_Grow_Plump_in_the_Night "For Girls Who Grow Plump in the Night")_ (1973).[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-21) This was followed later in the year by the announcement of a new album, _[Paradise Filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_Filter?action=edit&redlink=1 "Paradise Filter")_ (2013). The album was funded by a [PledgeMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PledgeMusic "PledgeMusic") campaign, scheduled for release on 24 February 2014. Hastings declared, "now you can be part of the whole process by pledging to support this project".[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-22)

On 1 December 2013, founding member Coughlan died, having been in poor health for some years.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-obit-19)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-23) His funeral took place in Canterbury on 20 December. A statement from the band stated, "his unique style of playing and wonderful character will be sorely missed."[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-24)

Caravan headlined the Rites of Spring festival (RoSfest) festival in Pennsylvania, USA from 2 to 4 May 2014.

In 2021, Jim Leverton left the band to return to his R&B roots. His replacement is [Lee Pomeroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Pomeroy "Lee Pomeroy").

## Musical style

Caravan are considered a key example of the [Canterbury scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_scene "Canterbury scene") genre.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-gruaniad-25) Their records generally indicate a jazz influence, and the group's lyrics have been described as whimsical and very "English", particularly during Richard Sinclair's tenure in the band.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEIrvin2007234-26) Following Sinclair's departure, the musical direction has been predominantly led by Pye Hastings, who preferred a softer [pop rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_rock "Pop rock") approach.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERomano2010193-14) Though the group share a common history with [Soft Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Machine "Soft Machine"), they have been considered more melodic and closer to folk music[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERomano2010191-27) and frequently displaying a sense of humour. The group were predominantly album-oriented, but Hastings believes the group recorded enough straightforward pop that could have been hit singles if the record company had taken sufficient interest.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-hastings-28)

Instrumentally, David Sinclair's fuzztone [Hammond organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ "Hammond organ") sound is a key ingredient of the early Caravan albums,[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-gruaniad-25) and his playing is the dominant instrument on them.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTEIrvin2007234-26) His musical palette subsequently expanded to include [synthesizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizer "Synthesizer").[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-FOOTNOTERomano2010193-14) Jimmy Hastings' woodwind playing and orchestral arrangements have also been a regular feature in the band's music since its inception.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band)#cite_note-waterloo-12):9
