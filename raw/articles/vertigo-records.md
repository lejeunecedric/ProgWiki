Vertigo Records is a British record label founded in 1969 by Philips/Phonogram (later Phonogram/Philips) as a specialist imprint for progressive rock, hard rock and experimental music, spearheaded by label manager Olav Wyper. The label debuted in 1969 with its iconic "swirl" logo - an optical illusion design conceived by executive Olav Wyper during a moment of inspiration in a rain-fogged car window, where he sketched expanding circles to evoke a hypnotic, dizzying effect aligned with the label's name and its focus on psychedelic and progressive music - and the lavish gatefold sleeves with psychedelic artwork became hallmarks of its early releases. The debut album was Colosseum's Valentyne Suite (1969). Vertigo signed a roster of groundbreaking acts, including Black Sabbath (whose 1970 self-titled debut marked the birth of heavy metal, and Paranoid became the label's first UK No.1), Uriah Heep, Gentle Giant (self-titled debut and Acquiring the Taste), Colosseum, Juicy Lucy, Jade Warrior, and later expansions to Status Quo, Thin Lizzy, Kraftwerk, and Dire Straits (whose Brothers in Arms sold over 30 million). The first releases came with a black and white spiral label, replaced with Roger Dean's spaceship design in 1973.
