Zeuhl (pronounced [zœl]; 'celestial') is a music genre, a hybrid of jazz fusion, symphonic rock and neoclassical music, established in 1969 by the French band Magma. The term comes from Kobaïan, the fictional language created by Magma's Christian Vander and Klaus Blasquiz, in which Zeuhl Wortz means approximately 'celestial force'. Zeuhl is dominated by rhythm - a pumping bass guitar and sometimes sluggish or flexibly playing drum kits - slow repetitive structures that build a hypnotic atmosphere, solo passages of high technical finesse, and vocal parts ranging from polyphonic choral movements (as in Carl Orff's Carmina Burana) to soloistic shrill intonation. Dominique Leone of Pitchfork called it "what you'd expect an alien rock opera to sound like." Vander has said zeuhl means "the spirit through matter" and is "the sound which you can feel vibrating in your belly." Originally applied solely to Magma, the term was extended to similar French and Japanese bands (Happy Family, Koenji Hyakkai, Ruins, and French band Zao) from the 1970s onward.
