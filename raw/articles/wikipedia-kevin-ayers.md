Musical artist

**Kevin Ayers** (16 August 1944 – 18 February 2013) was an English singer-songwriter who was active in the English [psychedelic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_music "Psychedelic music") movement. Ayers was a founding member of the psychedelic band [Soft Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Machine "Soft Machine") in the mid-1960s, and was closely associated with the [Canterbury scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_scene "Canterbury scene").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-3) He recorded a series of albums as a solo artist and over the years worked with [Brian Eno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Eno "Brian Eno"), [Syd Barrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syd_Barrett "Syd Barrett"), [Bridget St John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridget_St_John "Bridget St John"), [John Cale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cale "John Cale"), [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John "Elton John"), [Robert Wyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt "Robert Wyatt"), [Andy Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Summers "Andy Summers"), [Mike Oldfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Oldfield "Mike Oldfield"), [Nico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_(German_singer) "Nico (German singer)") and [Ollie Halsall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ollie_Halsall "Ollie Halsall"), among others. After living for many years in [Deià](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dei%C3%A0 "Deià"), [Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallorca "Mallorca"), he returned to the United Kingdom in the mid-1990s before moving to the south of France. His last album, _[The Unfairground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Unfairground "The Unfairground")_, was released in 2007.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-4) The British rock journalist [Nick Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Kent "Nick Kent") wrote: "Kevin Ayers and Syd Barrett were the two most important people in British pop music. Everything that came after came from them."[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-KA_Ob-5)

## Biography

### Early life

Ayers was born in [Herne Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herne_Bay "Herne Bay"), [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent "Kent"),[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-The_Guardian-4_July_2003-6) the son of [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") producer [Rowan Ayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowan_Ayers "Rowan Ayers").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-7) Following his parents' divorce and his mother's subsequent marriage to a British civil servant, Ayers spent most of his childhood in [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Malaya "Federation of Malaya").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-The_Sunday_Times-2_September_2007-8) Ayers returned to England at the age of 12, and attended [Simon Langton Grammar School for Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Langton_Grammar_School_for_Boys "Simon Langton Grammar School for Boys") in [Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury "Canterbury").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-9) In his early college years he became involved with the emerging [Canterbury scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_scene "Canterbury scene") when he joined the [Wilde Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilde_Flowers "Wilde Flowers"),[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-10) a band that included Robert Wyatt and [Hugh Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Hopper "Hugh Hopper"), as well as future members of [Caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band) "Caravan (band)"). This prompted him to start writing songs and singing.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Soft Machine

Ayers and Wyatt left the Wilde Flowers, later joining keyboardist [Mike Ratledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Ratledge "Mike Ratledge") and guitarist [Daevid Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daevid_Allen "Daevid Allen") to form [Soft Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Machine "Soft Machine"). Ayers switched to bass (and later both guitar and bass following Allen's departure from that group) and shared vocals with the drummer, Robert Wyatt. The group's sound contrasted between Ayers's baritone and Wyatt's tenor singing, plus a mix of [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music") and [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"). The band often shared stages (particularly at the [UFO Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_Club "UFO Club")) with [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd").[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-11) They released their debut single " [Love Makes Sweet Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Makes_Sweet_Music "Love Makes Sweet Music")" / " [Feelin' Reelin' Squeelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Makes_Sweet_Music "Love Makes Sweet Music")" in February 1967, making it one of the first recordings from the new British psychedelic movement.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-The_Sunday_Times-2_September_2007-8) Their debut album, _[The Soft Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Soft_Machine_(Soft_Machine_album) "The Soft Machine (Soft Machine album)")_, was recorded in the US for ABC/Probe and released in 1968. It is considered a classic of the genre.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-12)

### Solo career, 1969–2013

#### 1970–1976

After an extensive tour of the United States opening for the [Jimi Hendrix Experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimi_Hendrix_Experience "Jimi Hendrix Experience"), a weary Ayers sold his white [Fender Jazz Bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fender_Jazz_Bass "Fender Jazz Bass") to [Noel Redding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Redding "Noel Redding")[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-Ref-1-13) and retreated to the beaches of [Ibiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibiza "Ibiza") in Spain with [Daevid Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daevid_Allen "Daevid Allen") to recuperate.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-whatever-14) While there, Ayers went on a songwriting binge that resulted in the songs that would make up his first album, _[Joy of a Toy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_of_a_Toy "Joy of a Toy")_. The album was one of the first released on the new [Harvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvest_Records "Harvest Records") label, alongside Pink Floyd's _[Ummagumma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummagumma "Ummagumma")_. _Joy of a Toy_ established Ayers as a unique talent with music that varied from the circus march of the title cut, to the [pastoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoral "Pastoral") "Girl on a Swing", and the ominous "Oleh Oleh Bandu Bandong", based on a Malay folksong. Ayers's colleagues from Soft Machine backed him on one track, "Song for Insane Times", and on some cuts with Rob Tait, sometime [Gong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong_(band) "Gong (band)") drummer.

One product of the sessions was the single, " [Religious Experience (Singing a Song in the Morning)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singing_a_Song_in_the_Morning "Singing a Song in the Morning")", early recordings of which featured [Syd Barrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syd_Barrett "Syd Barrett") on guitar and backing vocals.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-Ref-1-13) The lead guitar that appears on the final mix was often thought to have been played by Barrett, even appearing on various Barrett bootlegs, but Ayers said that he played the solo, emulating Barrett's style. However, the 2004 CD reissue of _Joy of a Toy_ includes a mix of this song featuring Barrett's guitar as a bonus track.

Ayers was to all intents and purposes a member of Gong in 1971 when the band first toured the UK. He also played an instrumental role in [Steve Hillage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Hillage "Steve Hillage") appearing in Gong in 1972, while Steve was touring France as a member of Ayers's band.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-15)

A second album, _[Shooting at the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_at_the_Moon_(album) "Shooting at the Moon (album)")_, soon followed. For this, Ayers assembled a band that he called the Whole World, including a young [Mike Oldfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Oldfield "Mike Oldfield") on bass and occasionally lead guitar, [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde "Avant-garde") composer [David Bedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bedford "David Bedford") on keyboards and improvising saxophonist, [Lol Coxhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lol_Coxhill "Lol Coxhill"). Again Ayers came up with a batch of engaging songs interspersed with avant-garde instrumentals and a heavy dose of whimsy.

The Whole World was reportedly an erratic band live, and Ayers was not cut out for life on the road touring. The band broke up after a short tour, with no hard feelings, as most of the musicians guested on Ayers's next album, _[Whatevershebringswesing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whatevershebringswesing "Whatevershebringswesing")_, which is regarded as one of his best,[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-16) featuring the mellifluous eight-minute title track that would become Ayers's signature sound for the 1970s.

_[Bananamour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bananamour "Bananamour")_ was the fourth studio album by Kevin Ayers and it featured some of his most accessible recordings, including "Shouting in a Bucket Blues" and his whimsical tribute to Syd Barrett, "Oh! Wot A Dream". After _Whatevershebringswesing_, Ayers assembled a new band anchored by drummer Eddie Sparrow and bassist Archie Legget and employed a more direct lyricism. The centrepiece of the album is "Decadence", a portrait of [Nico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_(German_singer) "Nico (German singer)").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-17)

1974 was a watershed year for Ayers. In addition to releasing his most compelling music in this year, he helped provide other artists with access to a wider stage, most notably [Lady June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_June "Lady June") (June Campbell Cramer). The recording, titled _[Lady June's Linguistic Leprosy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_June's_Linguistic_Leprosy "Lady June's Linguistic Leprosy")_, made in a front room of Cramer's home in Vale Court, Maida Vale,[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-18) brought Lady June's spoken-word poetry together with the music and voice of Ayers, and also had contributions by [Brian Eno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Eno "Brian Eno") and [Pip Pyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pip_Pyle "Pip Pyle"). It was originally released on Ayers's own Banana Productions label (via Virgin/Caroline).

_[The Confessions of Dr. Dream and Other Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Confessions_of_Dr._Dream_and_Other_Stories "The Confessions of Dr. Dream and Other Stories")_ marked Ayers's move to the more commercial Island record label and is considered by many to be the most cohesive example of Ayersian philosophy. The production was expensive, with Ayers quoting the recording costs in a 1974 NME interview as exceeding £32,000[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-19) (a vast figure at the time). On this LP Mike Oldfield returned to the fold, and guitarist Ollie Halsall from progressive rock band [Patto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patto "Patto") began a twenty-year partnership with Ayers.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/CaleNicoAyersEno.png/500px-CaleNicoAyersEno.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:CaleNicoAyersEno.png) A collage of [John Cale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cale "John Cale"), [Nico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico "Nico"), Ayers, and [Brian Eno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Eno "Brian Eno") performing at London's Rainbow Theatre on 1 June 1974.

On 1 June 1974, Ayers headlined a heavily publicised concert at the [Rainbow Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Theatre "Rainbow Theatre"), London, accompanied by [John Cale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cale "John Cale"), Nico, [Brian Eno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Eno "Brian Eno") and [Mike Oldfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Oldfield "Mike Oldfield"). The performance was released by Island Records just 27 days later on a live LP entitled _[June 1, 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_1,_1974 "June 1, 1974")_. Tensions were somewhat fraught at the event since the night before John Cale had caught Ayers sleeping with his wife,[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-20) prompting him to write the bile-soaked paean "Guts" that appeared on his 1975 album _[Slow Dazzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_Dazzle_(album) "Slow Dazzle (album)")_.

In 1976, Ayers returned to his original label Harvest and released _[Yes We Have No Mañanas (So Get Your Mañanas Today)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_We_Have_No_Ma%C3%B1anas_(So_Get_Your_Ma%C3%B1anas_Today) "Yes We Have No Mañanas (So Get Your Mañanas Today)")_. The album was a more commercial affair. and secured Ayers a new American contract with [ABC Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_Records "ABC Records"). The LP featured contributions from [B.J. Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.J._Cole "B.J. Cole") and [Zoot Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoot_Money "Zoot Money"). That same year Harvest released a collection entitled _Odd Ditties_, that assembled a colourful group of songs that Ayers had consigned to single B-Sides or left unreleased.

#### The european years (1978–1992)

The late 1970s and 1980s saw Ayers as a self-imposed exile in warmer climes (Spain), a fugitive from changing musical fashions, and a hostage to chemical addictions. _[Rainbow Takeaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Takeaway "Rainbow Takeaway")_ was released in 1978 and _[That's What You Get Babe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That's_What_You_Get_Babe "That's What You Get Babe")_ in 1980. 1983's _[Diamond Jack and the Queen of Pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Jack_and_the_Queen_of_Pain "Diamond Jack and the Queen of Pain")_, Ayers's 10th solo album, was perhaps a low-point for Ayers. He was quoted in a 1992 BBC Radio 1 interview as saying he had "virtually no recollection of making those records", and that living in Deià was "a very bad move on my part. The social scene was very intense, a meat market of expatriates all flaunting themselves and on display. My career was going downhill".[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-21) Ayers released two more solo albums, 1984's _[Deià...Vu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dei%C3%A0...Vu "Deià...Vu")_ and 1986's _[As Close As You Think](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_Close_As_You_Think "As Close As You Think")_ to little attention. The road back was marked with 1988's prophetically titled _[Falling Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falling_Up_(Kevin_Ayers_album) "Falling Up (Kevin Ayers album)")_, which received his first positive press notices in years.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-The_Guardian-4_July_2003-6) In 1987 he also recorded a vocal track for Mike Oldfield's single, " [Flying Start](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_Start_(song) "Flying Start (song)")". The lyrics of this song contains many references to Ayers's life.

Despite the positive reception _Falling Up_ received, Ayers by this point had almost completely withdrawn from any public stage. An acoustic album _[Still Life with Guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Life_with_Guitar "Still Life with Guitar")_ recorded with [Fairground Attraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairground_Attraction "Fairground Attraction") surfaced in France on the [FNAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fnac "Fnac") label and was subsequently released throughout Europe. After a European Tour in April/May 1992 his musical partner Ollie Halsall suddenly died of a drugs-related heart attack.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-22) Collaborations with Ayers fanatics Ultramarine and with Liverpool's Wizards of Twiddly completed his output in the 1990s. The Wizards of Twiddly collaboration encompassed a couple of concert tours of the U.K./ Europe during 1995[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-23) and a resulting live album, 'Turn the Lights Down' \[Market Square Records, 1999\].

In 1993, Ayers toured the USA twice, usually performing solo with occasional guests, including [Daevid Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daevid_Allen "Daevid Allen"), who was also touring at the same time. Aside from a few New York shows in 1980 with Ollie Halsall, these tours were Ayers's first live performances in the US since 1968. In 1998 and 2000 he returned for two California mini-tours, performing in Los Angeles and San Francisco and backed by local musicians. The 2000 concerts had Ayers double-billed with [Gong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong_(band) "Gong (band)"). Longtime friend [John Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Altman_(composer) "John Altman (composer)") joined the Los Angeles band in 2000.

BBC DJ [John Peel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Peel "John Peel") wrote in his autobiography: "Kevin Ayers' talent is so acute you could perform major eye surgery with it."[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-24)

#### Later years (2000–2013)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Kevin_Ayers_%C3%A0_Carcassonne_en_2009.jpg/500px-Kevin_Ayers_%C3%A0_Carcassonne_en_2009.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kevin_Ayers_%C3%A0_Carcassonne_en_2009.jpg) Ayers in [Carcassonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcassonne "Carcassonne"), September 2009

In the late 1990s, Ayers was living the life of a recluse in the south of France. At the Sculpture Centre he met American artist Timothy Shepard, who had been invited to use studio space there, and the two became friends. Ayers started to show up at Shepard's house with a guitar, and by 2005 passed some new recordings on to Shepard, most taped on a cassette recorder at his kitchen table. The songs were by turns "poignant, insightful and honest", and Shepard, "deeply moved" by what he heard,[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-BBC6_Interview_September_2006-25) encouraged Ayers to record them properly for a possible new album.

Signing with London's [LO-MAX Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LO-MAX_Records?action=edit&redlink=1 "LO-MAX Records"), Shepard found equal enthusiasm for the demos and after making some tentative enquiries, discovered a hotbed of interest in Ayers's work amongst the current generation of musicians.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-Mojo_working-26) New York's [Ladybug Transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladybug_Transistor "Ladybug Transistor") set up rehearsals for a possible recording organised by band leader Gary Olson, and Kevin and Shepard flew out to New York. When the rehearsals gelled, the entourage, which had now swelled to include horn and string players, flew out to [Tucson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson "Tucson"), [Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona "Arizona"), where the first sessions were recorded in a dusty hangar known as Wavelab Studios.

With the tapes from the first sessions, Shepard set about getting Ayers to complete the album in the UK, where by now word had spread, and a host of musicians started gravitating to the studio. Shepard recounted meeting [Teenage Fanclub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Fanclub "Teenage Fanclub") at a [Go-Betweens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go-Betweens "Go-Betweens") party and hearing their passion for Ayers's music,[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-BBC6_Interview_September_2006-25) and wrote a letter to singer, guitarist [Norman Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Blake_(Scottish_musician) "Norman Blake (Scottish musician)"). _[Mojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojo_(magazine) "Mojo (magazine)")_ magazine reported that, within a couple of weeks, Ayers was in a Glasgow studio with Teenage Fanclub and a host of their like-minded colleagues, who had all assembled to work with their hero.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-Mojo_working-26) [Bill Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Wells_(musician) "Bill Wells (musician)") from the Bill Wells Trio rubbed shoulders with [Euros Childs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euros_Childs "Euros Childs") from [Gorky's Zygotic Mynci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorky's_Zygotic_Mynci "Gorky's Zygotic Mynci") and [Francis Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Reader "Francis Reader") from the [Trash Can Sinatras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trash_Can_Sinatras "Trash Can Sinatras").

Friends and peers from the past also visited the sessions. Robert Wyatt provided his eerie Wyattron in the poignant "Cold Shoulder", [Phil Manzanera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Manzanera "Phil Manzanera") contributed to the brooding "Brainstorm", [Hugh Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Hopper "Hugh Hopper") from Soft Machine played bass on the title track and [Bridget St John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridget_St_John "Bridget St John"), a British folk singer beloved of John Peel, duetted with Ayers on "Baby Come Home",[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-27) the first time they had sung together since 1970 on _Shooting at the Moon_. _[The Unfairground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Unfairground "The Unfairground")_ was released to critical acclaim[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-28) in September 2007.

Ayers died in his sleep on 18 February 2013 in [Montolieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montolieu "Montolieu"), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France"), aged 68.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-29)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-30)[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-31)[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-32)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-33)[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-34) He was survived by three daughters, Rachel, Galen and Annaliese, and his sister, Kate.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-KA_Ob-5) Rachel and [Galen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siskin_(band) "Siskin (band)") are also active as singers and musicians.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-35)[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers#cite_note-36)
