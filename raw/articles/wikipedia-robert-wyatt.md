Musical artist

**Robert Wyatt** (born 28 January 1945) is an English retired musician. A founding member of the influential [Canterbury scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_scene "Canterbury scene") bands [Soft Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Machine "Soft Machine") and [Matching Mole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matching_Mole "Matching Mole"),[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-:0-4) he was initially a kit drummer and singer before becoming [paraplegic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraplegic "Paraplegic") following an accidental fall from a window in 1973, which led him to abandon band work, explore other instruments, and begin a 40-year solo career.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1)

Wyatt was a key player during the formative years of British [jazz fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion "Jazz fusion"), [psychedelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelia "Psychedelia") and [progressive rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_rock "Progressive rock"), but his own work became increasingly interpretative, collaborative and politicised from the mid-1970s onwards. His solo music has covered a particularly individual musical terrain ranging from covers of [pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music "Pop music") [singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_(music) "Single (music)") to shifting, amorphous song collections drawing on elements of [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"), [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") and [nursery rhyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursery_rhyme "Nursery rhyme").

Wyatt retired from his music career in 2014, stating "there is a pride in \[stopping\], I don't want \[the music\] to go off."[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-uncutdec2014-5) He is married to English painter and songwriter [Alfreda Benge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfreda_Benge "Alfreda Benge").

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robert_Wyatt&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Wyatt was born in [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol "Bristol") on 28 January 1945.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-Larkin2011-6) His mother, [Honor Wyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honor_Wyatt "Honor Wyatt"), was a journalist with the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC"), and his father, George Ellidge, was an [industrial psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Psychology "Industrial Psychology"). Honor Wyatt was a cousin of [Woodrow Wyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wyatt "Woodrow Wyatt"), whose political stance influenced Robert in joining the [Communist Party of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Great_Britain "Communist Party of Great Britain").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-7) Wyatt had two half-brothers from his parents' previous marriages, Honor Wyatt's son, actor [Julian Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Glover "Julian Glover"),[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-8) and George Ellidge's son, press photographer [Mark Ellidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ellidge "Mark Ellidge").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-9) His parents' friends were "quite bohemian", and his upbringing was "unconventional".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-Nickson-10) Wyatt said "It seemed perfectly normal to me. My father didn't join us until I was six, and he died ten years later, having retired early with multiple sclerosis, so I was brought up a lot by women."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-Nickson-10) Wyatt attended the [Simon Langton Grammar School for Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Langton_Grammar_School_for_Boys "Simon Langton Grammar School for Boys"), Canterbury,[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-11) and as a teenager lived with his parents in [Lydden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydden "Lydden") near [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover "Dover"), where he was taught drums by visiting American jazz drummer George Neidorf. It was during this period that Wyatt met and became friends with expatriate Australian musician [Daevid Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daevid_Allen "Daevid Allen"), who rented a room in Wyatt's family home.

In 1962, Wyatt and Neidorf moved to [Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallorca "Mallorca"), living near the poet [Robert Graves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Graves "Robert Graves"). The following year, Wyatt returned to England and joined the [Daevid Allen Trio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daevid_Allen_Trio "Daevid Allen Trio") with Allen and [Hugh Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Hopper "Hugh Hopper"). Allen subsequently left for France, and Wyatt and Hopper formed [the Wilde Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wilde_Flowers "The Wilde Flowers"), with [Kevin Ayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers "Kevin Ayers"), [Richard Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Sinclair "Richard Sinclair") and [Brian Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Hopper "Brian Hopper"). Wyatt was initially the drummer in the Wilde Flowers, but following the departure of Ayers, he also became lead singer.

## Soft Machine and Matching Mole

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robert_Wyatt&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Soft Machine and Matching Mole")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/Robert_Wyatt_1967.jpg/250px-Robert_Wyatt_1967.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Robert_Wyatt_1967.jpg) Wyatt on [VPRO-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VPRO "VPRO"), September 1967

In 1966, the Wilde Flowers split into two bands— [Caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_(band) "Caravan (band)") and the [Soft Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Machine "Soft Machine")—and Wyatt, along with Mike Ratledge, was invited to join [Soft Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Machine "Soft Machine") by [Kevin Ayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers "Kevin Ayers") and [Daevid Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daevid_Allen "Daevid Allen"). Wyatt both drummed and shared vocals with Ayers, an unusual combination for a stage rock band.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1) In 1970, after chaotic touring, three albums and increasing internal conflicts in Soft Machine, Wyatt released his first solo album, _[The End of an Ear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_End_of_an_Ear "The End of an Ear")_, which combined his vocal and multi-instrumental talents with tape effects.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1) A year later, after becoming increasingly unhappy about his musical ideas being rejected by the others, Wyatt left Soft Machine.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-:0-4) He participated in the [fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion "Jazz fusion") bigband [Centipede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centipede_(band) "Centipede (band)"),[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-:0-4) performed at the [JazzFest Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JazzFest_Berlin "JazzFest Berlin")'s New Violin Summit, a live concert with violinists [Jean-Luc Ponty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Luc_Ponty "Jean-Luc Ponty"), [Don "Sugarcane" Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_%22Sugarcane%22_Harris "Don \"Sugarcane\" Harris"), [Michał Urbaniak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micha%C5%82_Urbaniak "Michał Urbaniak") and Nipso Brantner, guitarist [Terje Rypdal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terje_Rypdal "Terje Rypdal"), keyboardist [Wolfgang Dauner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Dauner "Wolfgang Dauner") and bassist [Neville Whitehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neville_Whitehead_(bassist) "Neville Whitehead (bassist)"),[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-12) and formed his own band [Matching Mole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matching_Mole "Matching Mole") (a pun, "machine molle" being French for 'Soft Machine'), a largely instrumental outfit that recorded two albums.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-:0-4)

### Accident

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robert_Wyatt&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Accident")\]

Matching Mole gradually disbanded, and Wyatt began writing material that would eventually appear on his second 'solo' album in Venice. He began to assemble a new band to record these numbers, but on 1 June 1973, during a birthday party for [Gong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong_(band) "Gong (band)")'s [Gilli Smyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilli_Smyth "Gilli Smyth") and June Campbell Cramer (also known as [Lady June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_June "Lady June")) at the latter's [Maida Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maida_Vale "Maida Vale") home, an inebriated Wyatt fell from a fourth-floor window and broke his spine.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-Johnson-13)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-Cumming-14) He was [paralysed from the waist down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraplegia "Paraplegia") and has used a [wheelchair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelchair "Wheelchair") for mobility ever since. On 4 November, [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd") performed two benefit concerts, in one day, at London's [Rainbow Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Theatre "Rainbow Theatre"), supported by Soft Machine, and compered by [John Peel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Peel "John Peel"). The concerts raised a reported £10,000 for Wyatt.

In a [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4 "BBC Radio 4") profile aired in 2012, Wyatt revealed that he and his wife [Alfreda Benge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfreda_Benge "Alfreda Benge") were also given generous help by friends of Benge's, including supermodel [Jean Shrimpton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Shrimpton "Jean Shrimpton"), who gave them a car, and actress [Julie Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Christie "Julie Christie"), who gave them use of a flat in London, which they later bought from her. In the same interview, Wyatt also observed that his accident probably saved his life: although he did not drink when he was younger, he quickly started to drink heavily while touring the United States in the late 1960s supporting [the Jimi Hendrix Experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jimi_Hendrix_Experience "The Jimi Hendrix Experience"), and that he often caroused with heavy-drinking colleagues such as [Mitch Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitch_Mitchell "Mitch Mitchell"), [Noel Redding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Redding "Noel Redding") and especially [Keith Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Moon "Keith Moon") (who introduced him to the practice of alternating shots of [tequila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tequila "Tequila") and [Southern Comfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Comfort "Southern Comfort")). By his own estimation, he was an alcoholic by the early 1970s, and he felt that, had the accident not intervened to change his lifestyle, his heavy drinking and reckless behaviour would have eventually killed him.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-15)

## Solo career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robert_Wyatt&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Solo career")\]

The injury led Wyatt to abandon the Matching Mole project, and his rock drumming (though he would continue to play drums and percussion in more of a "jazz" fashion, without the use of his feet). He promptly embarked on a solo career, and with musician friends (including [Mike Oldfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Oldfield "Mike Oldfield"), [Ivor Cutler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivor_Cutler "Ivor Cutler") and [Henry Cow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow "Henry Cow") guitarist [Fred Frith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Frith "Fred Frith")) released his solo album _[Rock Bottom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Bottom_(album) "Rock Bottom (album)")_ on 26 July 1974.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1) The album had been largely composed prior to Wyatt's accident, but during Wyatt's convalescence, he rethought the arrangements to adjust to his new circumstances, and many of the lyrics were completed during this period.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-:0-4) The album was met with mostly positive reviews.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-16)[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-19)

Two months later Wyatt put out a single, a [cover version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_version "Cover version") of " [I'm a Believer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I'm_a_Believer "I'm a Believer")", which hit number 29 in the UK chart.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1) Both were produced by Pink Floyd drummer [Nick Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Mason "Nick Mason"). There were strong arguments with the producer of _[Top of the Pops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_of_the_Pops "Top of the Pops")_ surrounding Wyatt's performance of "I'm a Believer", on the grounds that his use of a wheelchair "was not suitable for family viewing", the producer wanting Wyatt to appear in a wicker chair. Wyatt won the day and "lost his rag but not the wheelchair". A contemporary issue of _[New Musical Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Musical_Express "New Musical Express")_ featured the band (a stand-in acting for Mason), all in wheelchairs, on its cover. Wyatt subsequently sang lead vocals on Mason's first solo album _[Fictitious Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Mason's_Fictitious_Sports "Nick Mason's Fictitious Sports")_ (1981), a suite of songs composed by American jazz musician [Carla Bley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Bley "Carla Bley").

His follow-up single, a reggae ballad remake of [Chris Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Andrews_(singer) "Chris Andrews (singer)")'s hit " [Yesterday Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yesterday_Man "Yesterday Man")", again produced by Mason, was eventually given a low-key release, "the boss at Virgin claiming that single was 'lugubrious', the delay and lack of promotion denting Wyatt's chances of a follow-up hit."[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-20)

Wyatt's next solo album, _[Ruth Is Stranger Than Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Is_Stranger_Than_Richard "Ruth Is Stranger Than Richard")_ (1975), produced by Wyatt apart from one track produced by Mason, was more jazz-led, with [free jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_jazz "Free jazz") influences.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-:0-4) Guest musicians included [Brian Eno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Eno "Brian Eno") on guitar, synthesizer and "direct inject anti-jazz ray gun". Wyatt went on to appear on the fifth release of Eno's [Obscure Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obscure_Records "Obscure Records") label, _Jan Steele/John Cage: Voices and Instruments_ (1976), singing two [Cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cage "John Cage") songs.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-21)

Throughout the rest of the 1970s Wyatt guested with various acts, including [Henry Cow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow "Henry Cow") (documented on their _[Concerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerts_(Henry_Cow_album) "Concerts (Henry Cow album)")_ album), [Hatfield and the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_and_the_North "Hatfield and the North"), [Carla Bley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Bley "Carla Bley"), Eno, [Michael Mantler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Mantler "Michael Mantler"),[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-:0-4) and [Roxy Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roxy_Music "Roxy Music") guitarist [Phil Manzanera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Manzanera "Phil Manzanera"), contributing lead vocals to lead track "Frontera", from Manzanera's 1975 solo debut _Diamond Head_. In 1976 he was featured vocalist on Mantler's settings of the poems of [Edward Gorey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Gorey "Edward Gorey"), appearing alongside [Terje Rypdal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terje_Rypdal "Terje Rypdal") (guitar) [Carla Bley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Bley "Carla Bley") (piano, clavinet, synthesizer), [Steve Swallow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Swallow "Steve Swallow") (bass) and [Jack DeJohnette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_DeJohnette "Jack DeJohnette") (drums) on the album _The Hapless Child and Other Stories_.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-:0-4)

His solo work during the early 1980s was increasingly politicised, and Wyatt became a member of the [Communist Party of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Great_Britain "Communist Party of Great Britain").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-:0-4) In 1983, his original version of [Elvis Costello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Costello "Elvis Costello") and [Clive Langer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Langer "Clive Langer")'s [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War "Falklands War")-inspired song " [Shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding_(song) "Shipbuilding (song)")",[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-22) which followed a series of political cover-versions (collected as _[Nothing Can Stop Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothing_Can_Stop_Us_(album) "Nothing Can Stop Us (album)")_), reached number 35 in the UK Singles Chart,[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-UK_Chart_stats-23) having reached number 2 in [John Peel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Peel "John Peel")'s [Festive Fifty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festive_Fifty "Festive Fifty") for 1982-released tracks.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-:0-4) In 1984 Wyatt provided guest vocals, along with [Tracey Thorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracey_Thorn "Tracey Thorn") and Claudia Figueroa, on "Venceremos (We Will Win)", a song expressing political solidarity with Chilean people suffering under [Pinochet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinochet "Pinochet")'s military dictatorship, released as a single by UK soul-jazz dance band [Working Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_Week_(band) "Working Week (band)"), also included on their debut album released the following year.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-24)

In 1985 Wyatt released _[Old Rottenhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Rottenhat "Old Rottenhat")_, his first album of original songs since _[Ruth Is Stranger Than Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Is_Stranger_Than_Richard "Ruth Is Stranger Than Richard")_.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-:0-4) The album featured strongly political songs with relatively sparse arrangements played largely by Wyatt alone.

In the late 1980s, after collaborations with other acts such as [News from Babel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_from_Babel "News from Babel"), [Scritti Politti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scritti_Politti "Scritti Politti"), and Japanese recording artist [Ryuichi Sakamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryuichi_Sakamoto "Ryuichi Sakamoto") on his 1989 cover of [the Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rolling_Stones "The Rolling Stones")' " [We Love You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Love_You "We Love You")" which had additional backing vocals from [Brian Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Wilson "Brian Wilson"),[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-25) he and his wife [Alfreda Benge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfreda_Benge "Alfreda Benge") spent a sabbatical in Spain, before returning in 1991 with a comeback album _[Dondestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dondestan "Dondestan")_. His 1997 album _[Shleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shleep "Shleep")_ was also praised.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1)

Wyatt provided guest vocals on the [Ultramarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultramarine_(band) "Ultramarine (band)") album _[United Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdoms_(album) "United Kingdoms (album)")_, released in 1993.

In 1999 he collaborated with the Italian singer [Cristina Donà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Don%C3%A0 "Cristina Donà") on her second album _[Nido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nido_(album)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Nido (album) (page does not exist)")_. In the summer of 2000 her first EP _[Goccia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goccia?action=edit&redlink=1 "Goccia (page does not exist)")_ was released and Wyatt made an appearance in the video of the title track.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-ALLMUSIC-1)

Wyatt contributed "Masters of the Field", as well as "The Highest Gander", "La Forêt Rouge" and "Hors Champ" to the soundtrack of the 2001 film _[Winged Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Peuple_Migrateur "Le Peuple Migrateur")_. He can be seen in the DVD's Special Features section, and is praised by the film's composer [Bruno Coulais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Coulais "Bruno Coulais") as being a big influence in his younger days.

In June 2001, Wyatt was curator of the [Meltdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meltdown_(festival) "Meltdown (festival)") festival, and sang " [Comfortably Numb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comfortably_Numb "Comfortably Numb")" with David Gilmour at the festival. It was recorded on Gilmour's DVD _[David Gilmour in Concert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gilmour_in_Concert "David Gilmour in Concert")_.

In January 2003, [BBC Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Four "BBC Four") broadcast _Free Will and Testament_, a programme featuring performance footage of Wyatt with musicians Ian Maidman, Liam Genockey, [Annie Whitehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Whitehead "Annie Whitehead") and Janette Mason, and interviews with John Peel, Brian Eno, Annie Whitehead, Alfie and Wyatt himself.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-26) Later in 2003, the [Mercury Music Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Music_Prize "Mercury Music Prize") nominated album _Cuckooland_ was released.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-27)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/RobertWyatt_2006.jpg/250px-RobertWyatt_2006.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:RobertWyatt_2006.jpg) Wyatt in London, April 2006

In 2004 Wyatt collaborated with [Björk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rk "Björk") on the song "Submarine" which was released on her fifth album _[Medúlla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Med%C3%BAlla_(album) "Medúlla (album)")_. He sang and played cornet and percussion with [David Gilmour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gilmour "David Gilmour") on Gilmour's album _[On an Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_an_Island "On an Island")_, and read passages from the novels of [Haruki Murakami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haruki_Murakami "Haruki Murakami") for [Max Richter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Richter_(composer) "Max Richter (composer)")'s album _Songs from Before_. In 2006 Wyatt collaborated with [Steve Nieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Nieve "Steve Nieve") and [Muriel Teodori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muriel_Teodori "Muriel Teodori") on the opera _[Welcome to the Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_to_the_Voice "Welcome to the Voice")_ interpreting the character 'the Friend', both singing and playing pocket trumpet.

Wyatt released _[Comicopera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comicopera "Comicopera")_ in October 2007 on [Domino Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domino_Recording_Company "Domino Recording Company"), who went on to re-release _Drury Lane_, _[Rock Bottom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Bottom_(album) "Rock Bottom (album)")_, _[Ruth Is Stranger Than Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Is_Stranger_Than_Richard "Ruth Is Stranger Than Richard")_, _[Nothing Can Stop Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothing_Can_Stop_Us_(album) "Nothing Can Stop Us (album)")_, _[Old Rottenhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Rottenhat "Old Rottenhat")_, _[Dondestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dondestan "Dondestan")_, _[Shleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shleep "Shleep")_, _EPs_ and _[Cuckooland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuckooland "Cuckooland")_ on CD and vinyl the following year. In 2009 he appeared on the album _Around Robert Wyatt_ by the French Orchestre National de Jazz.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-28)

In 2008, Wyatt collaborated with [Hot Chip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Chip "Hot Chip") on the EP _Hot Chip with Robert Wyatt and Geese_.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-29)

Wyatt was one of the guest editors of [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4 "BBC Radio 4")'s _[Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Today_(BBC_Radio_4) "Today (BBC Radio 4)")_ programme, working on the 1 January 2010 programme.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-30) Among other things he advocated greater prominence for amateur choirs, and admitted to a preference for them over professional choirs "because there's a greater sense of commitment and meaning in their singing."[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-31)[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-32)

October 2014 saw the release of _Different Every Time: The Authorised Biography of Robert Wyatt_ by [Marcus O'Dair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_O'Dair "Marcus O'Dair"). In promotion of the book Wyatt appeared at _[the Wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wire_(magazine) "The Wire (magazine)")_'s "Off the Page" festival in Bristol on 26 September, and at the Queen Elizabeth Hall on 23 November. A companion compilation album, _Different Every Time – Ex Machina / Benign Dictatorships_ was released on 18 November 2014.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-33)

Wyatt performed the soundtrack to [Jimmy McGovern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_McGovern "Jimmy McGovern")'s 2014 BBC production, _[Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_(film) "Common (film)")_.

In an interview with _[Uncut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)")_ magazine in December 2014, Wyatt announced that he had "stopped" making music. He cited age and greater interest in politics as his reasons.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-uncutdec2014-5)

In January 2015 Wyatt's biography _Different Every Time_ was featured as [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4 "BBC Radio 4")'s _[Book of the Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_the_Week "Book of the Week")_, abridged by Katrin Williams and read by [Julian Rhind-Tutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Rhind-Tutt "Julian Rhind-Tutt").[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-34)

On 16 December 2016 Wyatt appeared at the [Brighton Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_Dome "Brighton Dome"), with [Paul Weller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Weller "Paul Weller") and [Danny Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Thompson "Danny Thompson"), in support of Labour Party leader [Jeremy Corbyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Corbyn "Jeremy Corbyn"), as the opening instalment of "People Powered: Concerts for Corbyn".[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-35)[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-36) It was Wyatt's most recent public appearance.

## Influence on other artists

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robert_Wyatt&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Influence on other artists")\]

The [Tears for Fears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tears_for_Fears "Tears for Fears") song " [I Believe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Believe_(Tears_for_Fears_song) "I Believe (Tears for Fears song)")" from their 1985 album _[Songs from the Big Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songs_from_the_Big_Chair "Songs from the Big Chair")_ was originally written by bandmember [Roland Orzabal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Orzabal "Roland Orzabal") for Wyatt, and is dedicated to him. As a further tribute to Wyatt, on the B-side of the single, Orzabal performs a cover version of "Sea Song", from the _[Rock Bottom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Bottom_(album) "Rock Bottom (album)")_ album. This recording later appeared on the compilation album _[Saturnine Martial & Lunatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturnine_Martial_&_Lunatic "Saturnine Martial & Lunatic")_ and the remastered versions of _Songs from the Big Chair_.

"Sea Song" was also covered by [Rachel Unthank and the Winterset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Unthanks "The Unthanks") on their 2007 album _[The Bairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bairns_(album) "The Bairns (album)")_, and _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_'s David Peschek said of the cover: "That's the best version of that I've ever heard".[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-37) In November 2011, [The Unthanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Unthanks "The Unthanks") released a live album, _[The Songs of Robert Wyatt and Antony & the Johnsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Songs_of_Robert_Wyatt_and_Antony_&_the_Johnsons "The Songs of Robert Wyatt and Antony & the Johnsons")_, and Wyatt is quoted on the cover of the album as saying "I love the idea. It makes me happy just thinking about it."

[Dev Hynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dev_Hynes "Dev Hynes") credited Wyatt's album, _Old Rottenhat_ as an influence on the song, "Take Your Time" from Hynes' 2018 album (as Blood Orange), _[Negro Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negro_Swan "Negro Swan")_.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-38)

### "Wyatting"

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robert_Wyatt&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: \"Wyatting\"")\]

The verb "Wyatting" appeared in some blogs and music magazines to describe the practice of playing unusual tracks, in particular songs from Wyatt's album _[Dondestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dondestan "Dondestan")_, on a pub jukebox to annoy the other pub goers. Wyatt was quoted in 2006 in _The Guardian_ as saying "I think it's really funny" and "I'm very honoured at the idea of becoming a verb."[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-39) When asked if he would ever try it himself, he said: "I don't really like disconcerting people, but even when I try to be normal I disconcert anyway."[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-40) However, Alfreda Benge said it made her angry "that Robert should be used as a means of clever dicks asserting their superiority in pubs ... It's so unlike Robert, because he's so appreciative of the strengths of pop music. So that, I think, is a real unfairness. The man who coined it, I should like to punch him in the nose."[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-41)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robert_Wyatt&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Wyatt is married to English painter and songwriter [Alfreda Benge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfreda_Benge "Alfreda Benge").[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-42)[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-43)

As of 2023, Wyatt had been diagnosed with [Lewy body dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewy_body_dementia "Lewy body dementia").[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt#cite_note-44)
