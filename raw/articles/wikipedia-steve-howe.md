Steve Howe (born 8 April 1947) is an English musician, best known as the guitarist and backing vocalist in the progressive rock band Yes across three stints since 1970. Howe's blend of acoustic and electric guitar helped shape the sound and direction of the band, leading to more commercial and critical success. Howe co-wrote many of their best-known songs and remained with the band until its initial dissolution in 1981, returning permanently in 1995; he is the band's longest-serving member.

In April 1970, Yes sought a new guitarist following the departure of Peter Banks. Howe auditioned and became a member in June. His first album with them was The Yes Album (1971). Howe's proficiency with a wide range of guitars, and his strong contributions to the songwriting, made him a prolific and essential part of the band's change in musical direction towards progressive rock. The Yes Album and Fragile include his solo acoustic pieces 'Clap' and 'Mood for a Day', and writing credits for 'Yours Is No Disgrace', 'Starship Trooper', and 'Roundabout'. To his guitar sound Howe added a unique approach to lap steel guitar on Close to the Edge (1972). He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Yes in 2017.
