**Henry Cow** were an English [experimental rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_rock "Experimental rock") [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_ensemble "Musical ensemble"), founded at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") in 1968 by multi-instrumentalists [Fred Frith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Frith "Fred Frith") and [Tim Hodgkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Hodgkinson "Tim Hodgkinson"). Henry Cow's personnel fluctuated over their decade together; alongside Frith and Hodgkinson, however, drummer [Chris Cutler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Cutler "Chris Cutler"), bassist [John Greaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Greaves_(musician) "John Greaves (musician)"), and bassoonist/oboist [Lindsay Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsay_Cooper "Lindsay Cooper") were important long-term members.

An inherent anti-commercial attitude kept them at arm's length from the mainstream [music business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_business "Music business"), enabling them to experiment at will. Critic [Myles Boisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myles_Boisen "Myles Boisen") writes, "\[their sound\] was so mercurial and daring that they had few imitators, even though they inspired many on both sides of the Atlantic with a blend of spontaneity, intricate structures, philosophy, and humor that has endured and transcended the ' [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_rock "Progressive rock")' tag."[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-allmusic-4)

While it was generally thought that Henry Cow took their name from 20th-century American composer [Henry Cowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cowell "Henry Cowell"),[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-8) this has been repeatedly denied by band members.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-calyx-HC-bio-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTECutler2009a21-10) According to Hodgkinson, the name "Henry Cow" was "in the air" in 1968, and it seemed like a good name for the band. It had no connection to anything.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-RoughGuide-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTECutler20066-12) In a 1974 interview, Cutler said the name was chosen because "\[i\]t's silly. What could be sillier than Henry Cow?"[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Murray-13)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Henry_Cow&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

### Early years

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Henry_Cow&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Early years")\]

[Fred Frith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Frith "Fred Frith") met [Tim Hodgkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Hodgkinson "Tim Hodgkinson"), a fellow student, in a blues club at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University "Cambridge University") in May 1968. Recognising their mutual open-minded approach to music, the two began performing together, playing a variety of musical styles including " [dada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dada "Dada") [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues")" and "neo-Hiroshima". One of Henry Cow's first concerts was supporting [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd") at the Architects' Ball at [Homerton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homerton_College "Homerton College"), [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge"), on 12 June 1968.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-HC-chronology-14)

In October 1968, Henry Cow expanded when they were joined by [Andy Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Powell "Andrew Powell") (bass guitar), [David Attwooll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Attwooll "David Attwooll") (drums)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Attwooll-15) and Rob Brooks (rhythm guitar). They performed with this line-up until December that year, when Frith, Hodgkinson and Powell split off from the rest of the group and became a trio. Powell at the time was studying music at [King's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge "King's College, Cambridge") under [Roger Smalley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Smalley "Roger Smalley"), the resident composer. Smalley was influential in Henry Cow's early development. He exposed them to a variety of new music from bands and musicians like [Soft Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Machine "Soft Machine"), [Captain Beefheart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Beefheart "Captain Beefheart") and [Frank Zappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Zappa "Frank Zappa"). Smalley also introduced them to the idea of writing long and complex musical pieces for rock groups.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-dtc-16) It was at this time that Henry Cow began writing music to challenge their collective ability to play, then using it to improve on themselves.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-pw-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-MM-18)

As a trio, with Frith on bass guitar, Powell on drums and Hodgkinson playing an organ that Frith and Powell had persuaded him to learn, Henry Cow performed a number of gigs on the university calendar, including the annual Architects' Ball and the Midsummer Common Festival, as well as a performance on the roof of a 14-storey building in Cambridge. In April 1969, Powell left and the band reverted to a duo, with Frith playing violin and Hodgkinson on keyboards and reeds. In October 1969 [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher "Philosopher") [Galen Strawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galen_Strawson "Galen Strawson") auditioned for the band. Later, Frith and Hodgkinson persuaded [bassist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassist "Bassist") [John Greaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Greaves_(musician) "John Greaves (musician)") to join the band; with the services of a couple of temporary drummers and then [Sean Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elastic_Band "The Elastic Band"), Henry Cow performed as a quartet for the next eight months. In May 1971, [Martin Ditcham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Ditcham "Martin Ditcham") replaced Jenkins on drums, and with this line-up they played at several events, including the [Glastonbury Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glastonbury_Festival "Glastonbury Festival") alongside [Gong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong_(band) "Gong (band)") in June 1971.

Ditcham left in July 1971, and it was not until September that year that the drummer's seat was filled again, this time by Chris Cutler. Responding to one of Cutler's adverts in _[Melody Maker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melody_Maker "Melody Maker")_, the band invited him to a rehearsal,[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-dtc-16) and it was only when Cutler joined that Henry Cow settled into a permanent core of Frith, Hodgkinson, Cutler and Greaves. The band then relocated to London, where they began an aggressive rehearsal schedule.

After having entered [John Peel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Peel "John Peel")'s "Rockortunity Knocks" contest in 1971, Henry Cow recorded a John Peel session for [BBC Radio 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_1 "BBC Radio 1") in February 1972. They later went on to record another session in October that year and a further three sessions between 1973 and 1975.

In April 1972, Henry Cow wrote and performed the music for Robert Walker's production of [Euripides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripides "Euripides")' _[The Bacchae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bacchae "The Bacchae")_. This involved an intense and demanding three-week period of concentrated work that changed the band completely. It was during this time that [Geoff Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Leigh "Geoff Leigh") on [woodwinds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodwind "Woodwind") joined and Henry Cow became a [quintet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintet "Quintet").

In July 1972, the band performed at the [Edinburgh Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_International_Festival "Edinburgh International Festival"), and wrote and performed music for a ballet with artist [Ray Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Smith_(artist) "Ray Smith (artist)") and the Cambridge Contemporary Dance Group at the [Edinburgh Festival Fringe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Festival_Fringe "Edinburgh Festival Fringe"). Smith appeared with Henry Cow at several of their early 1970s performances,[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-HC-chronology-14) to "add a dimension to the whole experience".[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTERomano2014%22Chapter_17_&#124;_Shock_to_the_System_&#124;_Henry_Cow_and_Rock_in_Opposition%22-19) Smith's acts included "set\[ting\] up an ironing board stage left and spen\[ding\] the whole evening ... quietly ironing" at the [Rainbow Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Theatre "Rainbow Theatre"), "read\[ing\] out short passages of discontinuous text between each piece of music" at the [Hammersmith Palais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammersmith_Palais "Hammersmith Palais"), and miming with a [glove puppet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glove_puppet "Glove puppet") at the [New London Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_London_Theatre "New London Theatre").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTERomano2014%22Chapter_17_&#124;_Shock_to_the_System_&#124;_Henry_Cow_and_Rock_in_Opposition%22-19) Smith later went on to do the "paint sock" art work for three of Henry Cow's LP covers.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTERomano2014%22Chapter_8_&#124;_Escape_Artists_%E2%80%93_Designing_and_Creating_Prog_Rock's_Wondrous_Visuals_&#124;_Henry_Cow:_''Legend''_(1973)%22-20)

Back in London, they started to organise a series of concerts and events under the names Cabaret Voltaire and Explorers' Club at the [Kensington Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Hall,_Kensington "Old Town Hall, Kensington") and the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics") respectively. Invited guests included [Derek Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Bailey_(guitarist) "Derek Bailey (guitarist)"), [Lol Coxhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lol_Coxhill "Lol Coxhill"), [Ivor Cutler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivor_Cutler "Ivor Cutler"), [Ron Geesin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Geesin "Ron Geesin"), [David Toop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Toop "David Toop"), [Lady June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_June "Lady June") and Smith.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-HC-chronology-14) Improvisers Bailey and Coxhill became "enthusiastic supporters" of Henry Cow and attended many of their concerts; Frith later stated that he was "strongly affected by their critical engagement and encouragement".[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTECutler2009a15-21) For the first time the band started getting some attention from the national music press.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPiekut201973-22) Reviewing the first Cabaret Voltaire event with [Kevin Ayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ayers "Kevin Ayers") in October 1972 in _[New Musical Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Musical_Express "New Musical Express")_, [Ian MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_MacDonald "Ian MacDonald") described Henry Cow as "one of the most resilient and obstinate of that range of groups normally ignored by the popular music press".[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-MacDonald-14Oct1972-23) This exposure, and a John Peel recording session in April 1973, led to the band signing with [Virgin Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Records "Virgin Records") in May 1973.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPiekut201975,_98-24)

### Unrest

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Henry_Cow&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Unrest")\]

Within two weeks of signing the contract, Henry Cow began recording their debut album _[Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legend_(Henry_Cow_album) "Legend (Henry Cow album)")_ (also known as _Leg End_) at Virgin's [Manor Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manor_Studios "Manor Studios") in [Oxfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxfordshire "Oxfordshire"). It took three weeks of hard work, but at the end they were able to handle the studio themselves, which would prove to be invaluable later in their career. The track " [Nine Funerals of the Citizen King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Funerals_of_the_Citizen_King "Nine Funerals of the Citizen King")", sung by the whole group, was Henry Cow's first overtly political statement.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Martens-25)

To promote its new signing, Virgin organised a UK tour for Henry Cow and [Faust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faust_(band) "Faust (band)"), who had also just signed to the label. During this tour, Henry Cow began preparing music for an unorthodox and provocative play, based on [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare "Shakespeare")'s _[The Tempest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tempest "The Tempest")_. Some of this music was used on their next record _[Unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unrest_(Henry_Cow_album) "Unrest (Henry Cow album)")_. In November 1973, members of the band participated in a live-in-the-studio performance of [Mike Oldfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Oldfield "Mike Oldfield")'s _[Tubular Bells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubular_Bells "Tubular Bells")_ for the BBC,[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-26) which was later released on the 2004 DVD edition of Oldfield's video compilation, _[Elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elements_%E2%80%93_The_Best_of_Mike_Oldfield_(video) "Elements – The Best of Mike Oldfield (video)")_.

During a tour of the Netherlands in December 1973, [Geoff Leigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Leigh "Geoff Leigh") left the group.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-dtc-16) Looking for more unusual instruments to draw them further away from rock and jazz,[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-CC-HC-27) Henry Cow asked [classically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_classical_music "European classical music") trained [Lindsay Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsay_Cooper "Lindsay Cooper") ( [oboe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oboe "Oboe"), [bassoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassoon "Bassoon")) to join. In early 1974 the group
began recording _Unrest_ at The Manor. Having only enough prepared material to fill one side of the LP, they developed a studio composition process that produced the second side.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-CC-HC-27)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/d/da/HenryCow%2BWyatt.jpg/330px-HenryCow%2BWyatt.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:HenryCow+Wyatt.jpg) Henry Cow with Robert Wyatt performing at the Piazza Navona, 1975. Left to right: Lindsay Cooper, Robert Wyatt, Dagmar Krause, Chris Cutler

In May 1974, Henry Cow were on tour again around England and Europe with [Captain Beefheart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Beefheart "Captain Beefheart"). It was during this tour that they realised they were becoming a conventional rock band, playing the same material night after night. Their music was no longer a challenge and they were becoming complacent. After some serious deliberating, they asked Cooper to leave and fulfilled their last outstanding concert obligations (a tour of the Netherlands) as a [quartet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartet "Quartet"). Without Cooper they were forced to abandon much of their learned material and worked on a 35–40 minute piece Hodgkinson had written that later became the politically charged " [Living in the Heart of the Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_in_the_Heart_of_the_Beast "Living in the Heart of the Beast")" on their third album, _[In Praise of Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Praise_of_Learning "In Praise of Learning")_.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Martens-25)

In November 1974, [avant-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-pop "Avant-pop") group [Slapp Happy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slapp_Happy "Slapp Happy") ( [Anthony Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Moore "Anthony Moore"), [Peter Blegvad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Blegvad "Peter Blegvad"), [Dagmar Krause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagmar_Krause "Dagmar Krause")) invited Henry Cow to record with them on their second album for Virgin. The result was _[Desperate Straights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desperate_Straights "Desperate Straights")_, an almost entirely Slapp Happy–composed album that surprised critics, considering how dissimilar the two groups were.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Mills-28) The success of this venture prompted a merger of the two bands, and in early 1975 they recorded _In Praise of Learning_ at The Manor. The merger ended in April 1975 when Moore and Blegvad left. Krause remained with Henry Cow, which effectively spelled the end of Slapp Happy.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Ankeny-29)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPiekut2019196-30)

Having made guest appearances on both the Henry Cow/Slapp Happy albums, Cooper rejoined in April 1975 and Henry Cow became a [sextet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextet "Sextet"). In May 1975 they embarked on a concert tour with [Robert Wyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt "Robert Wyatt") in England, France and Italy to launch _In Praise of Learning_ and Wyatt's new album, _[Ruth Is Stranger Than Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Is_Stranger_Than_Richard "Ruth Is Stranger Than Richard")_.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPiekut2019212-31)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-HC-chronology-14) This was followed by a period of almost constant touring across Western Europe that continued until Henry Cow disbanded in 1978.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Gross-32)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-HC-chronology-14)

### Europe

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Henry_Cow&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Europe")\]

Henry Cow's music was challenging and uncompromising and this often led to them being accused of deliberately making it unapproachable.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-dtc-16) In a review of _Unrest_ in _New Musical Express_ on 15 June 1974, Neil Spencer called the band "determinedly inaccessible".[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPiekut2019141-33)[\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-34) As a result, Henry Cow were virtually ignored in their own country. Virgin Records too, having started dropping experimental groups in favour of commercial ones, now showed little to no interest in the band. The group continuously considered whether to continue or not; there certainly were no economic inducements. Cutler said, "We had to make what amounted to political decisions about the organization of the group and its relation to the commercial structures, and this was bound to be reflected in the music too."[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Macan-p1973-35) Henry Cow's [anti-capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-capitalist "Anti-capitalist") stance[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-36) was brought on partly out of necessity rather than choice. They began working outside the [music industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_industry "Music industry") and did everything for themselves. They abandoned agencies and managers and stopped looking for approval from the music press. Henry Cow quickly became self-sufficient and self-reliant.

Virtual exiles from their own country, they made mainland Europe their second home where they (and their music) were well received. After a concert in Rome in July 1975, Henry Cow remained behind with their truck/bus/mobile home and began meeting local musicians, including [progressive rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_rock "Progressive rock") band [Stormy Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stormy_Six "Stormy Six"), and the PCI ( [Italian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Communist_Party "Italian Communist Party")). The PCI offered them concerts at Festa de L'Unità (large open-air fairs that run every summer all over Italy), and they joined Stormy Six's [L'Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Orchestra?action=edit&redlink=1 "L'Orchestra (page does not exist)"), a musicians' co-operative in [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan "Milan"). Each contact they made led to more contacts and soon doors opened for Henry Cow all over Europe.

While rehearsing for an upcoming tour of Scandinavia in March 1976, John Greaves left the band to start working on the _[Kew. Rhone.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kew._Rhone. "Kew. Rhone.")_ project with Peter Blegvad, and Dagmar Krause withdrew due to ill health. Committed to the tour, Henry Cow had to perform as a [quartet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartet "Quartet") (Hodgkinson, Frith, Cooper and Cutler) and adjust their music accordingly. They took the radical option and abandoned composed material completely in favour of pure [improvisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_improvisation "Free improvisation").

In May 1976, Henry Cow compiled a double LP _[Concerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerts_(Henry_Cow_album) "Concerts (Henry Cow album)")_ for a new Norwegian underground label Compendium (re-released later on the budget Virgin sub-label [Caroline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Records "Caroline Records")). For the first time, they did everything themselves: the mastering, cover design, cutting, pressing and manufacturing. The album included an excerpt from one of several concerts performed with guest artist [Robert Wyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wyatt "Robert Wyatt") in 1975.

Henry Cow began auditioning for a [bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassist "Bassist") player and found [Georgie Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgie_Born "Georgie Born"), a classically trained [cellist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellist "Cellist") and [improviser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improviser "Improviser"). Even though she had never played bass guitar before,[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTECutler2009b34-37) she joined the band in June 1976 and tuned her bass in 5ths like a cello with a lower C.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTECutler2009b5-38) In the interim, the band's compositions, including a new Hodgkinson epic with the working title of " [Erk Gah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erk_Gah "Erk Gah")", grew more complex.

Henry Cow returned to London in early 1977, where they merged with the entire [Mike Westbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Westbrook "Mike Westbrook") Brass Band and folk singer [Frankie Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankie_Armstrong "Frankie Armstrong") to form [the Orckestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Orckestra "The Orckestra"). They played their first concert at the Moving Left Revue at [The Roundhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roundhouse "The Roundhouse") in London and then at the [Open Air Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Air_Theatre "Open Air Theatre") in [Regent's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent's_Park "Regent's Park"). The Orckestra later went on to tour in France, Italy and Scandinavia (extracts from some of these performances were released in 2006 on a [CD-single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD-single "CD-single") included in the _[Henry Cow Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow_Box "Henry Cow Box")_). At more or less the same time they set up [Music for Socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_for_Socialism?action=edit&redlink=1 "Music for Socialism (page does not exist)") and its May Festival. It had been three years since Henry Cow had performed more than one concert a year in their own country. In an attempt to break the apathy that seemed to be discouraging anyone from wanting to put them on, they tried to organise a small alternative tour themselves, but abandoned it after 11 concerts when they started losing money: clearly nothing had changed.

Their contract with Virgin Records had now become a burden to both Henry Cow and Virgin: none of Henry Cow's records were licensed or distributed in the countries in which they spent all their time playing, and Henry Cow were not making any money for Virgin. Henry Cow needed to record again but Virgin refused to give them studio time at [The Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Manor_Studio "The Manor Studio"). When Henry Cow referred to the contract ("one month at a first class studio"),[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-CC-HC-27) Virgin Records (in October 1977) agreed to cancel it.

By now, Krause's health had deteriorated to such an extent that touring became impossible for her and she decided to leave the group, although she agreed to sing on Henry Cow's next album. The recording of this album was to begin at Sunrise studios in [Kirchberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirchberg,_St._Gallen "Kirchberg, St. Gallen"), Switzerland in January 1978. However, a group meeting one week before threw into question the material planned for it, the aforementioned "Erk Gah" in particular. Cutler and Frith hurriedly wrote a set of songs which, along with some of the planned material, were duly recorded. On returning to London, another meeting was convened to question the predominance of songs on the album. The group agreed that the songs would be released separately by Cutler and Frith, while the instrumentals would be released later by Henry Cow. This decision, however, spelled the end of the band. Cutler, Frith and Krause released the songs, with four extra tracks recorded at [David Vorhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Vorhaus "David Vorhaus")'s Kaleidophon Studio in London, as _[Hopes and Fears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopes_and_Fears_(Art_Bears_album) "Hopes and Fears (Art Bears album)")_ under the name [Art Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Bears "Art Bears"), crediting the rest of Henry Cow as guests. Later that year Henry Cow returned to Sunrise, by then without Dagmar Krause and Georgie Born, to record their last album, _[Western Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Culture_(album) "Western Culture (album)")_, an instrumental. [Annemarie Roelofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annemarie_Roelofs "Annemarie Roelofs") had joined the band two months before the split and plays on the album as well.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-HC-chronology-14)

### Rock in Opposition

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Henry_Cow&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Rock in Opposition")\]

Main article: [Rock in Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_in_Opposition "Rock in Opposition")

Henry Cow agreed to disband as a permanent group, but did not announce the fact immediately. They continued for another six months, creating a new set of material (recorded later to complete _Western Culture_) and revisited for the last time all the places that had supported them over the years.

In March 1978, Henry Cow invited four European groups, [Stormy Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stormy_Six "Stormy Six") (Italy), [Samla Mammas Manna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samla_Mammas_Manna "Samla Mammas Manna") (Sweden), [Univers Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univers_Zero "Univers Zero") (Belgium) and [Etron Fou Leloublan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etron_Fou_Leloublan "Etron Fou Leloublan") (France), to come to London and perform in a festival Henry Cow had organised called [Rock in Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_in_Opposition "Rock in Opposition"), or RIO. Throughout Europe, Henry Cow had encountered many " [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_rock "Progressive rock")" groups refusing to bow to the hegemony of American and British [rock music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music"). Instead they drew on non-American music sources, such as local [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") and 20th-century " [classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_classical_music "European classical music")" or "art music", and often sang in their own languages. As was the case with Henry Cow, these groups struggled to survive: record companies were not interested in their music. Although these groups and Henry Cow were musically diverse, what they had in common was: (1) their independence and opposition to the established [rock music business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll "Rock and roll"); and (2) a determination to pursue their own work regardless.

After the festival, RIO was formalised as an organisation with a [charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter "Charter") whose aim was to represent and promote its members. RIO thus became a collective of bands united in their opposition to the [music industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_industry "Music industry") and the pressures to compromise their music.

Henry Cow's last concert was held in [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan "Milan") on 25 July 1978. A final performance scheduled at the Annual World Youth Festival in [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba "Cuba") never materialised.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-pw-17) In August they returned to the Sunrise studios to complete _Western Culture_, after which the band officially announced their break-up in the press, stating that "… although the group as a commodity, as a name, ceases to exist the work of the group will go on …"[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-calyx-HC-bio-9)

_Western Culture_ was released on Henry Cow's own Broadcast label. Shortly afterward, Chris Cutler launched [Recommended Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recommended_Records "Recommended Records"), his own independent label and non-commercial record distribution network.

A partial Henry Cow reunion occurred in 1993 when Hodgkinson, Cutler, Cooper and Krause came together to record " [Hold to the Zero Burn, Imagine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hold_to_the_Zero_Burn,_Imagine "Hold to the Zero Burn, Imagine")" for Hodgkinson's solo album, _[Each in Our Own Thoughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Each_in_Our_Own_Thoughts "Each in Our Own Thoughts")_. The song was formerly known as "Erk Gah" and composed by Hodgkinson for, and performed by, Henry Cow. When asked in 1998 about a possible Henry Cow reunion concert, Frith replied, "Forget it! We're all much too busy."[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Warburton-39)

In December 2006, Cutler, Frith and Hodgkinson performed together for six shows, two each night from Friday 15 to Sunday 17, at [The Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stone_(music_space) "The Stone (music space)") in New York City; they were joined at the first Friday set by [Hugh Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Hopper "Hugh Hopper") of [Soft Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Machine "Soft Machine").[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-40)[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-41) This was their second concert performance since Henry Cow disbanded in 1978, after an earlier reunion show in London in 1986.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Frith and Hodgkinson also performed improvised duo concerts in 1990; extracts of the concerts were released in 1992 as _[Live Improvisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Improvisations "Live Improvisations")_.

In May 2019, the [Michel Edelin Quintet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Edelin_Quintet?action=edit&redlink=1 "Michel Edelin Quintet (page does not exist)")\[ [fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel%20Edelin "fr:Michel Edelin")\] with John Greaves released _[Echoes of Henry Cow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echoes_of_Henry_Cow "Echoes of Henry Cow")_, an album of variations on Henry Cow compositions and other music.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Edelin-48) Aymeric Leroy wrote in the liner notes that it should not be seen as a Henry Cow [tribute album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribute_album "Tribute album"), but rather "echoes (much transformed during its long journey through time, space, memory and the mysterious twists and turns of the creative process) in \[Edelin's\] own musical inner world".[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Edelin-48)

## Music

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Henry_Cow&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Music")\]

Henry Cow's music included elaborately scored pieces (often with complex [time signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_signature "Time signature")), [tape loops and manipulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reel-to-reel_audio_tape_recording "Reel-to-reel audio tape recording"), "flat-out [free improvisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_improvisation "Free improvisation")" and [songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song "Song").[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-AllAboutJazz-56) It incorporated elements of [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"), [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music"), [contemporary classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_classical_music "Contemporary classical music") and the [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde "Avant-garde"). Dagmar Krause's vocals added another dimension to their sound, giving it a dramatic, almost [Brechtian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brecht "Brecht") flair. Music journalists at the time often underestimated the formal compositional element of their music,[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-pw-17) while others simply dismissed it as being "inaccessible".[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-dtc-16)

John Kelman wrote at _[All About Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_About_Jazz "All About Jazz")_ that "Henry Cow represented a new kind of classical chamber music; one where spontaneity was a partial component, and the instrumentation used created textures that defied those looking for tradition and convention."[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-AllAboutJazz-56) Edward Macan in his 1997 book _Rocking the Classics: English Progressive Rock and the Counterculture_ described Henry Cow's music as "highly eclectic" and said that their pieces often included "furious [atonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atonal "Atonal") instrumental passages with no discernable melodic contour or key center, impossibly complex shifting meters alternating with freely [ametric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ametric "Ametric") sections with no definable beat or regular recurring rhythms, and jagged, _[sprechstimme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprechstimme "Sprechstimme")_-like vocal lines that blur the line between song and speech."[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Macan-p1973-35)

Trond Einar Garmo described Henry Cow's music as [avant-garde rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde_music "Avant-garde music").[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarmo20203-57) Writing in his book, _Henry Cow: An Analysis of Avant Garde Rock_ (2020), Garmo stated:

> I have chosen to use the term 'avant-garde rock' for Henry Cow's music. The word 'avant-garde' is usually associated with art that is 'difficult', 'incomprehensible', and to some extent 'meaningless'. The actual meaning of the word, however, is 'vanguard'. Avant-garde artists are, therefore, artists who find new directions and expand the boundaries of the art form in which they operate. And it is in this sense that I have used the word. Avant-garde rock represents the most experimental and radical trends within rock.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarmo20203-57)

Henry Cow's music was challenging, not only to the listener, but also to the band themselves. They often composed pieces to challenge their own capabilities. Some of their music was scored beyond the conventional ranges of their instruments, necessitating that they "reinvent their instruments" and learn how to play them in completely new ways.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-pw-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-MM-18) Frith explained in a 1973 interview, "What we've done is to literally teach ourselves to ... compos\[e\] music which we could not initially, play. Because of that attitude, we can go on forever. It's a self-generative concept which gives us a sense of purpose most groups simply don't have."[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-MacDonald-7Apr1973-58) And yet their music may not have been as good as it could have been. Henry Cow conducted their affairs as a [collective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective "Collective") and all decisions, including those related to their music, had to be approved by the group. Cutler said at a conference on "Composition and Experimentation in British Rock 1967–1976" in Italy in 2005 that Henry Cow had a rule that "the composer no longer owned the composition once the band had started to work on it."[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Cutler-2005-59) In a 1998 interview Frith said that this may have led to much of Henry Cow's material being "watered down" rather than strengthened.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Warburton-39) He felt that "this ... was a big mistake, and a lot of our best ideas may not have been fully realised as a result of it."[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-Warburton-39) Cutler wrote that when Art Bears was formed in 1978, he and Frith decided there would be "no discussions; if someone had an idea, they put it to tape. Then we'd listen and it would be immediately clear if it worked, didn't work or could work if pursued."[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-CC-AB-60)

Henry Cow were largely a live band, yet of the original six albums they made, only one, _[Concerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerts_(Henry_Cow_album) "Concerts (Henry Cow album)")_ gave a glimpse of their live performances. In January 2009 [Recommended Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recommended_Records "Recommended Records") released _[The 40th Anniversary Henry Cow Box Set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_40th_Anniversary_Henry_Cow_Box_Set "The 40th Anniversary Henry Cow Box Set")_, a nine-CD plus one-DVD collection of over 10 hours of previously unreleased and mostly live recordings made between 1972 and 1978, over four hours of which was improvised. This offered, "for the first time," according to Kelman, "a comprehensive account of Henry Cow's breadth and depth."[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-AllAboutJazz-56)

### Timeline

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Henry_Cow&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Timeline")\]

Notes: before Sean Jenkins joined, the band auditioned several other drummers. According to Fred Frith, between 1969 and 1971, the band played more as a trio than with a drummer.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cow#cite_note-HC-chronology-14) From November 1974 to April 1975, Henry Cow merged with [Slapp Happy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slapp_Happy "Slapp Happy") to form one group. The band's final studio album, _[Western Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Culture_(album) "Western Culture (album)")_, was released in 1979 after the group had split up.
