Neo-progressive rock (commonly abbreviated neo-prog) is a subgenre of progressive rock that developed in the UK in the early 1980s. The genre's most popular band, Marillion, achieved mainstream success in the decade.

Neo-prog is characterised by deeply emotional content, dramatic lyrics and theatrical imagery. The music is mostly the product of careful composition, relying less on improvised jamming, and relies on clean, melodic electric guitar solos with keyboards. Its main influences are the first wave of progressive rock — early Genesis, Camel, and to a lesser extent Van der Graaf Generator and Pink Floyd — as well as funk, hard rock and punk.

Famous neo-prog albums include Marillion's Script for a Jester's Tear and Misplaced Childhood, Pallas's The Sentinel, Pendragon's Fly High Fall Far, Twelfth Night's Art and Illusion, Solstice's Silent Dance, IQ's The Wake and Quasar's Fire in the Sky. Neo-prog peaked in the mid-1980s; each of the British neo-progressive bands released material around 1984. Marillion achieved arena status in the UK and were the only neo-prog band to do so, producing a No.1 album with Misplaced Childhood (1985) and eight UK Top 10 albums between 1983 and 1994. Marillion pioneered fan-funded music with Anoraknophobia.

Steve Hogarth replaced Fish in 1989. Marillion have released 20 studio albums.
