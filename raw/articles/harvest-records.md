Harvest Records is a British-American record label belonging to Capitol Music Group, originally created by EMI in 1969 to market progressive rock, and to compete with Philips' Vertigo and Decca's Deram labels and the independent Island label. Harvest was initially under the direction of Malcolm Jones, and distributed in North America by EMI's affiliate Capitol Records. In the 1970s the label primarily released progressive rock recordings by British artists including Pink Floyd (their UK label), Deep Purple, Syd Barrett, Kevin Ayers, Roy Harper, Barclay James Harvest, Be-Bop Deluxe, Wire, and others, with the aim of exploring progressive rock, underground, and psychedelic music. As the 1970s drew to a close the focus shifted with punk/new wave releases (The Saints, Wire, Thomas Dolby, Be-Bop Deluxe) and new romantic acts (Duran Duran on Harvest America), plus Iron Maiden's first three US albums. Later, much of Harvest's European back catalogue was divested by Universal Music after its takeover of EMI as part of the Parlophone Label Group. Capitol relaunched the label in 2013 with a new roster (TV on the Radio, Death Grips, White Lies, etc.).
