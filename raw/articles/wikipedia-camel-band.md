**Camel** are an English progressive rock band formed in Guildford, Surrey, in 1971. The band was formed by guitarist Andrew Latimer, drummer Andy Ward, and bassist Doug Ferguson, who recruited keyboardist Peter Bardens. They made their live debut under the name Camel in London on 4 December 1971. Because they had primarily functioned as an instrumental backing group, they lacked a dedicated lead vocalist. They cultivated a reputation as a proficient live ensemble that blended progressive rock and jazz rock.
The band became a prominent fixture of the 1970s progressive rock movement, achieving a dedicated cult following and critical acclaim with a series of conceptual and thematic releases, including Mirage (1974), the instrumental breakthrough The Snow Goose (1975), and Moonmadness (1976). During the late 1970s and early 1980s, the group pivoted toward a more commercial, jazz-oriented direction on records such as Rain Dances (1977) and Nude (1981), while undergoing rapid lineup alterations. The band temporarily dissolved in late 1984. Latimer subsequently relocated to the United States and established the independent label Camel Productions, under which he reformed the band in 1991.
Their music has been widely cited as a primary influence on a later generation of progressive rock and metal artists, including Marillion, Opeth, and Steven Wilson.
