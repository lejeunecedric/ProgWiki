The Rickenbacker 4001 is an electric bass that was manufactured by Rickenbacker from 1961 to 1986. Designer Roger Rossmeisl created the 4001 as a two-pickup 'deluxe' version of Rickenbacker's first production bass, the single-pickup model 4000. The 4001 sported the same unique body shape and 'crested wave' headstock of the 4000, with a neck-through-body construction as a distinguishing feature from most other basses.

Many notable rock bassists have played a 4001 or 4001S, including Paul McCartney, John Entwistle, Roger Waters, and Chris Squire. In the 1970s, 4001 and 4001S sales were given a boost by Yes bassist Chris Squire, who popularized using roundwound strings on his bass to create a bright, twangy tone. Squire's tone inspired a number of other progressive rock bassists to use the 4001, including Geddy Lee of Rush, who bought one with his advance following the band signing its first record deal in 1974. The 4001 would finish the 1970s as Rickenbacker's bestselling instrument. Rickenbacker produced a signature 'Chris Squire' model bass (4001CS) for Yes's Squire.
