Stephen Richard Hackett (born 12 February 1950) is an English guitarist who gained prominence as the lead guitarist of the progressive rock band Genesis from 1971 to 1977. Hackett contributed to six Genesis studio albums, three live albums, seven singles and one EP before he left to pursue a solo career. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Genesis in 2010.

In December 1970, Hackett placed an advertisement in Melody Maker. It was responded to by Genesis lead vocalist Peter Gabriel. Genesis had lost founding guitarist Anthony Phillips and sought a new replacement. Hackett's first recording with Genesis was Nursery Cryme (1971). His guitar work is featured prominently through solos on "The Musical Box", "The Return of the Giant Hogweed" and "The Fountain of Salmacis". He became an early proponent of the guitar tapping technique normally attributed to Eddie Van Halen.

Foxtrot (1972) includes Hackett's guitar solo composition "Horizons", which he based on a Suite for Cello by Bach. The song "Firth of Fifth" on Selling England by the Pound contains one of Hackett's most well-known guitar solos.

In 1977 Hackett left Genesis to pursue a solo career. He co-founded the supergroup GTR with Steve Howe in 1986, and has released a large body of solo work exploring progressive rock, blues, world music, classical and other styles.
