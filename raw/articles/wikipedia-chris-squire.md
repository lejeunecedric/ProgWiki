Chris Squire (4 March 1948 - 27 June 2015) was an English musician, singer and songwriter, best known as the bassist, backing vocalist, and only constant member of the progressive rock band Yes until his death in 2015. He was widely regarded as the dominant bassist among the English progressive rock bands, influencing peers and later generations of bassists with his incisive sound and elaborately contoured, melodic bass lines. His name was associated with his trademark instrument, the Rickenbacker 4001 (British model RM1999).

After finding work selling guitars at a Boosey & Hawkes shop in Regent Street, Squire used his staff discount to purchase a new bass, a Rickenbacker 4001, in 1965. In May 1968 he was introduced to Jon Anderson, and the two found they shared common musical interests including Simon & Garfunkel and the idea of vocal harmonies. Squire was the only member to play on each of the 21 studio albums released by Yes from 1969 to 2014 alert.

Squire and Howe would supply backing vocals in harmony with Anderson on songs such as 'South Side of the Sky' and 'Close to the Edge'. Squire described his playing on 'The Remembering (High the Memory)' from Tales from Topographic Oceans (1973) as 'one of the nicest things I think I've ever played'. Squire died on 27 June 2015, and in 2020 Rolling Stone ranked him the 18th greatest bass player of all time.
