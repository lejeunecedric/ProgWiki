_The Dark Side of the Moon_ is the eighth studio album by the English progressive rock band Pink Floyd, released on 1 March 1973. Developed during live performances, it was conceived as a concept album that would focus on the pressures faced by the band during their arduous lifestyle, and also deal with the mental health problems of former band member Syd Barrett. The record builds on ideas explored in Pink Floyd's earlier work, while limiting the extended instrumentals that characterised the band's earlier work. The group employed multitrack recording, tape loops, and analogue synthesisers, including experimentation with the EMS VCS 3 and a Synthi A.
_The Dark Side of the Moon_ has received widespread critical acclaim and is often featured in publication listings of the greatest albums of all time. It brought international wealth to all four band members. It is certified 14x platinum in the United Kingdomaine and topped the US Billboard 200 chart, where it has charted for 996 weeks. By 2013 the album had sold over 45 million copies worldwide, making it the band's best-selling release and the best-selling album of the 1970s. In 2012, the album was selected for preservation in the United States National Recording Registry for being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".
